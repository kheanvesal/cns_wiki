# Text-to-Speech

Technology that synthesizes spoken output from text, used in the corpus as a **compensatory reading aid** — reading text aloud so the reader can decode visually while hearing it.

## Role in the corpus
Only [[sources/validating-personalized-visual-auditory-parameters-dyslexia]] (Lorusso et al., 2024) exercises TTS as a measured variable. See [[concepts/multimodal-and-audio-support]].

## Parameters tested
- **Rate/speed:** levels tested, with **0.8** (slightly slower than natural) preferred by both groups.
- **Pitch:** with **0.6** (deeper/less acute than default) preferred by both groups.
- **Voice identity:** children selected a preferred voice, then identified which nonword the TTS pronounced most accurately (syllable-level check of intelligibility). Baseline for comparison was the **default** voice.

## Findings
- Pitch choice differed significantly by group: Somers' `D=0.143, p=0.005`.
- Rate differed marginally: `D=0.150, p=0.054`.
- Personalized voice significantly improved **dictation** in the AD group (`Z=2.05, p=0.020`) but **not** in the TD group (`Z=−0.09, p=0.463`).
- The authors caution these parameters are **not generalizable** to all individuals, "especially in the AD group."

## Caveats
- Dictation confounds auditory and visual channels; TTS-specific effects are not cleanly isolated.
- Prior literature finds little objective evidence for TTS improving **reading accuracy** (vs. comprehension) for students with reading/language difficulties; this study is among the few to test accuracy directly.
- Any specific rate/pitch optimum is tied to the synthesis quality available at the time; modern TTS may shift these values.
