# The Effects of Interletter and Line Spacing on Reading Speed, Comprehension, and Perceived Comfortability

**Citation.** Kristjánsdóttir, S., & Gunnarsson, V. (2024). *The effects of interletter and line spacing on reading speed, comprehension, and perceived comfortability.* BSc thesis, University of Iceland (advisor: T. Kristjánsson).

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension + reading speed + comfort (mobile phone reading).

**Question/aim.** Effect of letter spacing and line spacing on reading speed, comprehension, and reading experience on a mobile device.

**Method.** Experimental, reading off a smartphone; manipulated letter spacing and line spacing; measured speed, comprehension, self-reported comfort.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Interletter (letter) spacing; line spacing (leading).

**Key findings.**
- No significant difference in reading speed or comprehension across letter- or line-spacing conditions.
- Significant difference in **comfortability** across text conditions (self-report).

**Effect direction & size.** Null for speed/comprehension; significant for perceived comfort. Sizes not reported.

**Limitations/caveats.** Student BSc thesis; mobile-only; measures diverge (behavioral null vs. self-report significant) — a classic method-type disagreement.
