# Concept Mapping Style in EFL Reading Comprehension

**Citation.** Andoko, B. S., Hayashi, Y., & Hirashima, T. (2019). An Analysis of Concept Mapping Style in EFL Reading Comprehension from the Viewpoint of Paragraph Structure of Text. *Information and Systems in Education*, 18(1), 63–68.

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension (EFL).

**Question/aim.** Whether map-making in Kit-Build (KB) vs. Scratch-Build (SB) concept mapping follows paragraph structure rather than sentence order, and how this relates to comprehension.

**Method.** Behavioral/log analysis of the map-making process; compares KB mapping (components pre-supplied) vs. SB mapping (learner builds nodes/links). Prior work in the series used delayed comprehension tests two weeks out.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Not typographic — the "style" is the *knowledge-representation* format (concept map vs. sentence-by-sentence reading) and paragraph structure of the source text.

**Key findings.**
- Sentence-by-sentence reading treats text as separate sentences, not a whole structure; less proficient EFL readers monitor at word level, more proficient use inter-sentential information.
- KB mapping better supports recall of understood information than SB on delayed tests.
- Map-making groups by paragraph-level meaning sets rather than strict sentence order.

**Effect direction & size.** KB > SB for delayed recall (from cited companion study); effect sizes not reported here.

**Limitations/caveats.** Short note; small design; narrow to EFL + concept-mapping tooling. Peripheral to typography/layout but relevant to text *structure* and comprehension strategy.
