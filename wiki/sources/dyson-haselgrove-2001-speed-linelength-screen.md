# The Influence of Reading Speed and Line Length on the Effectiveness of Reading from Screen

**Citation.** Dyson, M. C., & Haselgrove, M. (2001). The influence of reading speed and line length on the effectiveness of reading from screen. *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies*, 54, 585–612. doi:10.1006/ijhc.2001.0458.

> **File note.** Stored as `effectief lezen van scherm.pdf` (Dutch filename; paper is in English).

**Reading task(s).** Reading/skimming from screen.

**Question/aim.** Effects of two reading speeds (normal, fast) and different line lengths on reading effectiveness (comprehension) from screen.

**Method.** Experiment crossing reading speed × line length; comprehension measures.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Line length (characters per line); reading speed.

**Key findings.**
- A **medium line length** (~55 characters per line) supported the best comprehension.
- Fast reading reduced comprehension (speed–accuracy tradeoff), interacting with line length.
- Reinforces **cpl** as the key line-length mediator (cf. [[sources/layout-on-screen]]).

**Effect direction & size.** Medium line length optimal; fast reading lowers comprehension.

**Limitations/caveats.** Early-2000s screens; specific text set.
