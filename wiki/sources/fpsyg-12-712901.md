# Levels of Reading Comprehension in Higher Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**Citation.** de-la-Peña, C., & Luque-Rojas, M. J. (2021). Levels of Reading Comprehension in Higher Education: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 12, 712901.

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension (population-level, higher education).

**Question/aim.** What proportion of university students reach optimal performance at each comprehension level (literal, inferential, critical, organizational).

**Method.** Systematic review + random-effects meta-analysis of proportions; studies 2010–Mar 2021 (WoS, Scopus, Medline, PsycINFO). 7 studies, total N=1,044; meta-regression for moderators; heterogeneity via I².

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** None — establishes baseline distribution of comprehension attainment (context for the corpus, not a style manipulation).

**Key findings.**
- Optimal attainment: literal 56%, inferential 33%, critical 22%, organizational 22%.
- Significantly more students reach optimal *literal* than the deeper levels.
- Very high heterogeneity (I² up to ~99%) → interpret cautiously.

**Effect direction & size.** Proportions with wide CIs; high heterogeneity.

**Limitations/caveats.** Only 7 studies; extreme heterogeneity; no style/layout link — use as a baseline anchor.

**Connections.** [[concepts/comprehension-levels]] · [[syntheses/Comprehension]] — deeper comprehension levels are rarer, so style factors that free cognitive resources matter most there.
