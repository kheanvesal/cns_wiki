# The Inattentive On-Screen Reading: Reading Medium Affects Attention and Reading Comprehension Under Time Pressure

**Citation.** Delgado, P., & Salmerón, L. (2021). The inattentive on-screen reading: Reading medium affects attention and reading comprehension under time pressure. *Learning and Instruction*, 71, 101396.

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension under time pressure (screen vs. paper).

**Question/aim.** How reading medium and time-frame affect on-task attention (mind-wandering), metacognitive calibration, and comprehension.

**Method.** Experiment; N=140 undergraduates; 2×2 medium (print vs. screen) × time-frame (free vs. pressured); measured mind-wandering, prediction of performance, comprehension.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Medium (print vs. screen); time-frame.

**Key findings.**
- **In-print** readers mind-wandered less under time pressure than free time (better task adaptation); **on-screen** readers did not adapt.
- Screen reading associated with more inattention and weaker comprehension under time pressure.
- Supports a "shallowing"/inattention account of screen reading.

**Effect direction & size.** Print > screen for attention adaptation and comprehension under pressure.

**Limitations/caveats.** Undergraduates; single text set; self-reported mind-wandering probes.
