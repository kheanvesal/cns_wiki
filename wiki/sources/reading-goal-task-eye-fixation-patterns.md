# Effects of Reading Goal and Reading Task on Eye Fixation Patterns

**Citation.** Hyönä, J., & Kaakinen, J. K. (2011). Effects of Reading Goal and Reading Task on Eye Fixation Patterns. *Studies of Psychology and Behavior* (心理与行为研究), 9(1), 24–34. (Received 2010-10-31; University of Turku.)

> **Resolved via OCR.** The `pdftotext` extraction was font-encoding-corrupted; authors, venue, and findings below confirmed by OCR of the original.

**Reading task(s).** Cross-task: reading for comprehension vs. proofreading; reading-goal manipulation (skimming-adjacent).

**Question/aim.** How reading **goal** (perspective) and reading **task** shape eye guidance during reading.

**Method.** Review of two of the authors' eye-tracking studies: (1) reading-**perspective** effects on eye behavior; (2) **proofreading vs. reading-for-comprehension**. Contrasts eye-movement evidence with the self-report/recall/think-aloud tradition (Lorch et al.; Narvaez, van den Broek & Ruiz 1999).

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Reading goal/perspective and task (not typography); text content familiarity.

**Key findings.**
- Both **initial word encoding** and the **later sentence-integration** ("wrap-up") stage are influenced by reading perspective; early effects appeared in **saccadic programming** and **fixation times**, primarily when reading **familiar-content** text.
- Task effects (proofreading vs. comprehension) appear **very early** in the processing time line; both **temporal and spatial** aspects of eye movements are affected.
- Readers make a **global adjustment** to eye behavior based on reading task/goal.

**Effect direction & size.** Early, global eye-movement adjustments by goal/task (review; magnitudes in the underlying studies).

**Limitations/caveats.** A short **review of two studies** (not new data); Chinese-journal venue. Directly cross-links to [[sources/journal-of-educational-psychology-1999]] (which it cites) on the eye-movement vs. self-report/think-aloud divergence.
