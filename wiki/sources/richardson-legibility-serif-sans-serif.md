# The Legibility of Serif and Sans Serif Typefaces: Reading from Paper and Reading from Screens

**Citation.** Richardson, J. T. E. *The Legibility of Serif and Sans Serif Typefaces: Reading from Paper and Reading from Screens.* SpringerBriefs in Education, Springer.

**Reading task(s).** Legibility across tasks (serif vs. sans debate).

**Question/aim.** Whether serif or sans serif typefaces are more legible, on paper and on screens.

**Method.** Book-length critical review of the empirical serif-vs-sans literature (historical to modern).

**Style/layout variables (reviewed).** Typeface class: serif vs. sans serif; medium (paper vs. screen).

**Key findings.**
- The long-assumed advantage of serif type for print (and sans for screen) is **not robustly supported**; differences are small and often confounded (size, spacing, exposure).
- Legibility differences between serif and sans are minor once other variables are controlled.
- Medium matters less than commonly claimed for typeface class.

**Effect direction & size.** Serif vs. sans: negligible/inconsistent once confounds controlled.

**Limitations/caveats.** Review; heterogeneous underlying studies and eras.
