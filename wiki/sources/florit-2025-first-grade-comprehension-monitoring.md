# Florit et al. (2025) — first-grade reading across paper, computer, tablet

## Citation
Florit, E., De Carli, P., Rodà, A., Cain, K., & Mason, L. (2025). Reading from paper, computers, and tablets in the first grade: The role of comprehension monitoring. *Computers and Education Open*, 8, 100243. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeo.2025.100243

## Reading task(s)
**comprehension** (primary), with comprehension monitoring (metacognition) as a second outcome.

## Question / aim
Test whether the **screen inferiority effect** for informational texts — previously established in university students — is evident in **beginner readers**, and whether it relates to comprehension monitoring.

## Method
- **Design:** Within-subjects repeated measures, linear mixed models with random effects; control-variable and supplementary models.
- **N:** **58 first graders**, M_age = 6.8. No child had cognitive impairments or was at risk for learning difficulty.
- **Materials:** One narrative + one informational (descriptive) text in Italian in three media; digital on 17" laptop and tablet. **Font size held constant across all media, including print.** No time limit (self-paced).
- **Controls:** Italian standardized *Prove MT-3-Clinica*; reading accuracy via 112 words + 48 non-words, reliability by McDonald's omega; medium preference pre-test.
- **Measures:** BEHAVIORAL — comprehension accuracy on main-point, literal, inferential questions; inconsistency detection as the comprehension-monitoring task. SELF-REPORT — medium preference. **No eye-tracking.**

## Style/layout variables manipulated
Reading medium (paper / laptop / tablet); text genre (narrative vs descriptive); font size constant; no time pressure.

## Key findings
- **No medium main effect on any comprehension outcome** — main point, literal, and inferential all **ps > 0.15**. This is the paper's headline null: **no screen inferiority effect in beginner readers.**
- Genre had a significant main effect on all three outcomes (ps ≤ 0.01; **η² = .02–.05**).
- **Medium × genre interaction was significant for main point and literal but not inferential:** main point η² = .02; literal η² = .04; **inferential p = .69**.
- **The tablet advantage is localized and genre-specific:** main-point answers were higher for **descriptive texts read on tablet than on paper**.
- Inferential comprehension favored **narrative over descriptive** text, independent of medium.
- **Comprehension monitoring related to main-point and literal comprehension regardless of medium**, and had a greater effect on descriptive than narrative comprehension at the inferential level.
- No floor/ceiling effects in the monitoring task (M = 4.78, SD = 2.97, range 0–8).

## Effect direction & size if reported
| Contrast | Outcome | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Paper vs screen | all three | **Null**, ps > 0.15 |
| Paper vs tablet | main point | Null overall; **tablet > paper** for descriptive texts only |
| Genre × medium | main point / literal | Significant, η² = .02 / .04 |
| Genre × medium | inferential | Null, p = .69 |

## Limitations/caveats
- Single first-grade sample, N = 58, **two texts only**; Italian materials.
- Interaction η² values are very small (.02–.04) and **effect sizes are reported with 90% confidence intervals, not 95%** — liberal by convention, and a direct obstacle to comparison with the 95% CIs elsewhere in this literature.
- Supplementary multiple comparisons are relegated to Tables S1/S2.
- The comprehension-monitoring task **confounds metacognitive monitoring with text-inconsistency detection** — it cannot separate "did I notice a contradiction" from "did I realize I might not have understood."
- Not powered as a medium-effect study; null results should not be read as equivalence.

## Connections
- **Direct counter-evidence to the screen-inferiority position**, and the youngest sample in the corpus to test it. Triangulates with the pooled literature's boundary conditions: the medium effects Kong's corpus found were concentrated in longer texts and specific screen types, and this study used short self-paced texts with constant font size.
- **Reverses the tablet direction** relative to the pooled estimate — tablet > paper here, while Kong's non-significant screen-type moderator (b = .22, p = .41) leaves tablet behavior unresolved. **Do not cite this as evidence of a change over time**: Kong's own Year trend is non-significant (p = .09) and cannot support a trajectory claim. See [[concepts/screen-vs-paper]] and the contradictions section of [[syntheses/Comprehension]].
- **Genre as a moderator is new relative to the rest of the screen literature here.** Mangen found *no* genre × medium interaction (p = .707); Florit finds a small one confined to main-point and literal questions. Same nominal variable, different age group, different answer — see [[concepts/text-structure]] and [[concepts/comprehension-levels]].
- **Comprehension monitoring predicting comprehension regardless of medium** is a within-study replication of the dissociation in [[sources/halamish-elbaz-2020-childrens-comprehension-metacomprehension]], where monitoring *failed* to detect the medium-driven comprehension loss. Together: monitoring tracks comprehension but does not track medium. See [[concepts/metacognitive-calibration]].
