# Lu, Li, Lu, Song, Yin & Zhong (2011) — visual search under information overload

## Citation
Lu, W., Li, M., Lu, S., Song, Y., Yin, J., & Zhong, N. (2011). Visual search strategy and information processing mode: an eye-tracking study on web pages under information overload. In L. Qi (Ed.), *ISIA 2010*, CCIS 86, pp. 153–159. Springer-Verlag. **No DOI.**

## Reading task(s)
**search** (visual search with a known target). Not reading or comprehension.

## Question / aim
How does **information overload** change (a) the visual **search strategy** — which information form users attend to — and (b) the **information processing mode** (parallel vs serial) when searching a webpage? Prior web visual-search work had covered form and layout but "few … about information quantity."

## Method
- **Design:** Eye-tracking study, **two-group BETWEEN-subjects design** with unequal n (no within-subject manipulation, no randomization to group mentioned).
- **N:** **130** undergraduates/postgraduates, half female, **native Chinese speakers**, right-handed, skilled internet users. **Group 1 (non-overload) n = 50**, age 21–25 (M 23.0, SD 1.3). **Group 2 (overload) n = 80**, age 20–27 (M 23.2, SD 1.3).
- **Information overload operationalized as page real estate at constant density:** both groups used the **same font, font size and line space**, so Group 2 carried **≈2× the information quantity**. Group 1 = **single page, no scrollbar, fits on one screen → 10 pages**. Group 2 = **double page with a vertical scrollbar, only half visible → 20 pages** (the same 5 locations replicated on the lower half).
- **Materials:** 10 Chinese web pages on varied topics. **Targets: 10 total = 5 texts (3–6 Chinese characters, company names) + 5 pictures (company logos)**, arranged at 5 locations (upper-left, lower-left, center, upper-right, lower-right), duplicated across halves in Group 2. Target form described in Chinese on a pre-page. 17-in LCD, 1024×768.
- **Apparatus:** **Tobii T120 at 120 Hz**, via **Tobii Studio**.
- **Measures:** BEHAVIORAL — search time (page appearance → click on target). EYE-TRACKING — scanpath "contrails"; distribution of the **earliest 20 fixation points** split by form; average fixation duration (fixation duration ÷ fixation count); fixation count. **Pupil size is mentioned as available but was NOT measured.** No regression or saccade-amplitude metrics. **No self-report.**
- **Theoretical mapping imposed by the authors:** ↑ average fixation duration ⇒ **serial** processing; ↑ fixation count ⇒ **parallel** processing.

## Key findings
- **Search time: Group 1 = 11.40 s, Group 2 = 17.42 s, p < .000** (as printed) — doubled information quantity significantly reduces search efficiency. This is the empirical basis for labelling Group 2 "overload," and it matches Hornof and Rau.
- **The search strategy is picture-oriented in BOTH conditions, and overload amplifies rather than changes it.** Earliest-20-fixation distribution: **picture 12.2 → 12.9, p < .05; text 7.8 → 7.1, p < .000.** Picture fixations exceed text in both conditions and the gap widens.
- **Average fixation duration: 0.37 s → 0.41 s, p < .05** ⇒ overload switches participants to **more serial processing**.
- **Fixation count: 31.16 → 34.93, p < .01** — but the rise is only **12.1%** while information quantity rose **≈100%**.
- **Normalized fixation rate: ≈31.16 per page under non-overload vs ≈17.47 per page under overload** ⇒ under overload participants **reduce parallel processing per unit of information**.
- **Scanpath under overload:** participants search an area carefully, move to another, and **rarely come back** — interpreted as inhibition of return serving cognitive economy.
- **A disconfirmed hypothesis, reported as such:** the authors predicted a "fast saccade" mode under overload (faster saccades, shorter fixations, more parallel processing). **The opposite pattern occurred.**
- **Design recommendation:** under overload, present important information **as a picture**, and prefer deep/serial region-by-region scanning over wide parallel sweeps.

## Effect direction & size if reported
p-values are printed on bar charts — **no F or t statistics, no effect sizes, no confidence intervals, and no n-per-cell for the fixation analyses.** Directionally: search time +53% under overload (11.40 → 17.42 s); fixation count +12.1% against ≈100% more information; average fixation duration 0.37 → 0.41 s.

## Limitations/caveats
- **Between-subjects with unequal groups (50 vs 80)** — the overload effect is confounded with differences between participant samples, and no randomization is mentioned.
- **The "overload" manipulation is inseparable from page length and scrolling.** It changes physical page height and requires scrolling, so any effect may be a scrolling/navigational effect rather than a pure information-quantity effect. **Scroll behaviour was never measured.**
- Figures-only reporting: no test statistics, no effect sizes, no CIs for the central claims.
- **Form is confounded with item properties** — "picture" = corporate logo vs "text" = company name; familiarity, word length, and visual distinctiveness differ systematically.
- No accuracy measure, no comprehension measure, no reading-speed covariate; fixation-detection threshold unstated.
- Chinese-only participants and Chinese-language stimuli — **the reading-direction confound noted at [[sources/li-2009-web-layout-information-forms-locations]]** applies here too. Very short proceedings paper; no DOI.

## Connections
- **Same research group, apparatus, 5-zone layout, and target set as [[sources/li-2009-web-layout-information-forms-locations]]**; together they are the corpus's most systematic treatment of web layout × search, and this paper supplies the quantity dimension that source explicitly lacks.
- **Independent corroboration of picture superiority in search** — from a different experiment with different N. That matters because it is the only *replicated* layout finding in this cluster: form (picture < text fixation duration) holds in both papers. See [[concepts/graphic-type]] and [[concepts/visual-search]].
- **Its serial/parallel processing mapping is the corpus's most explicit statement of an attentional *mechanism* for a layout effect**, and it is the natural mechanistic companion to [[concepts/satisficing-foraging]] (which predicts the same directional shift under time pressure) and [[concepts/rational-search-theory]].
- **Disconfirmed "fast saccade" prediction is worth keeping** — it is a case where a plausible mechanism was tested and refuted within the same paper, and [[syntheses/when-measures-disagree]] should carry it as a model of how to report a null hypothesis.
- **The overload-as-scroll confound is a live evidence gap**: no source in the corpus separates quantity from scrolling. Flagged in [[concepts/scrolling-vs-paging]].
