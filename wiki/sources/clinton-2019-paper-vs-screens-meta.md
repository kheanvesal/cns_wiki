# Reading from Paper Compared to Screens: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**Citation.** Clinton, V. (2019). Reading from paper compared to screens: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Research in Reading*, 42(2), 288–325. doi:10.1111/1467-9817.12269.

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension + reading time + metacognitive calibration (medium comparison).

**Question/aim.** Consolidate evidence on reading performance, reading time, and metacognition for paper vs. screen.

**Method.** Systematic review + meta-analysis; 7 databases; RCT-style experiments 2008–2018; 29 reports / 33 studies with fundamental-reading-skill participants.

**Style/layout variables manipulated (reviewed).** Presentation **medium** (paper vs. screen).

**Key findings.**
- Reading **comprehension** was better from paper than screens (small but reliable advantage), especially for informational texts.
- **Metacognitive calibration** (predicting one's performance) tends to be worse on screens (overconfidence).
- Reading **time** differences less consistent.

**Effect direction & size.** Small paper advantage for comprehension; calibration worse on screen.

**Limitations/caveats.** 2008–2018 window; heterogeneous devices; publication-bias considerations.
