# Bresó-Grancha, Jorques-Infante & Moret-Tatay (2022) — digital- vs print-easy texts

## Citation
Bresó-Grancha, N., Jorques-Infante, M. J., & Moret-Tatay, C. (2022). Reading digital- versus print-easy texts: a study with university students who prefer digital sources. *Psicologia: Reflexão e Crítica*, 35, 10. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41155-022-00212-4

## Reading task(s)
**comprehension** (primary), with reading time and lexical recognition as process measures.

## Question / aim
Examine reading time and lexical-decision performance on digital versus print, framed by the **shallowing hypothesis**, in university students who *prefer* digital sources — a sample selected against the usual print-advantage expectation.

## Method
- **Design:** Within-participant, counterbalanced order; two tasks per participant (reading comprehension task then lexical decision task). Non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-ranks with rank-biserial correlation, plus Bayesian paired analysis (BF₁₀) as convergent evidence.
- **N:** **40** university students (25 women, 15 men), M_age = 18.90 (SD 1.51). Spanish native speakers, normal/corrected vision, **all self-reported a preference for digital over print**.
- **Materials:** Two stories from prior literature with matching comprehension questions ("The Wind"). Lexical decision task from 120 Spanish words (60 high-frequency, mean 146.7/million; 60 low-frequency, mean 10.2/million) plus 120 pseudowords, split into two lists, 2,500 ms response window. DMDX for digital timing; the paper version required physically crossing out words.
- **Measures:** BEHAVIORAL — RCT accuracy, RCT reading time, LDT accuracy. **Lexical RTs were deliberately not the dependent variable** (medium-driven) — only accuracy was used for word recognition. No eye-tracking. SELF-REPORT preference was a **selection criterion, not an outcome**.

## Style/layout variables manipulated
Reading medium (digital vs print) on **easy** texts only. Response modality co-varied with medium (button press vs hand-crossing). **No difficult-text condition exists in this study.**

## Key findings
- **Print reading was dramatically slower than digital:** Z = −5.05, p < .001, rank-biserial **r = .91**, 95% CI [.84, .96]. The largest medium effect anywhere in this cluster of papers.
- **Comprehension is reported as significant but is internally incoherent:** Z = −1.16, p < .001, r = −.50, CI [−.72, −.20]. **A |Z| of 1.16 cannot yield p < .001 under a signed-ranks test** (it implies p ≈ .25).
- **The abstract contradicts the Results**, stating "no statistically significant differences were found in RCT accuracy" while the Results report p < .001.
- **The word-recognition result shows the same defect:** called "statistically significant" (Z = −3.84, p < .001) with r = −.04 and CI [−.37, .30] spanning zero.
- Correlation structure: RCT accuracy correlated across media (rho = .608, p < .01). Digital RCT time correlated positively with digital accuracy (rho = .39, p < .05) but print RCT time correlated **inversely** with accuracy in both media (rho = −.339, p < .05; rho = −.263, p < .01).
- Bayesian analysis: robust evidence for reading time and word recognition; for comprehension only "not anecdotal" — i.e. weak.

## Effect direction & size if reported
Reading time: print much slower, r = .91, p < .001. Comprehension: **inconclusive** (abstract null vs Results p < .001 with impossible Z). Word recognition: reported significant but CI spans zero.

## Limitations/caveats
- **Systematic reporting defect, not a one-off typo:** significance judgments track Z while the paper's own confidence intervals contradict them on two of three outcomes. Any citation must carry this caveat.
- **Self-selected digital-preferring sample** — cannot generalize to print-preferring readers, and confounds medium with preference.
- **Easy texts only** — no difficult-text condition, so it cannot speak to comprehension under cognitive load.
- **Response modality varies with reading medium** (button vs hand-crossing): a pure confound.
- N = 40; single easy story pair.

## Connections
- **The strongest evidence in the corpus that digital reading is substantially *faster*** (r = .91). This is in tension with the pooled speed null in [[sources/kong-seo-zhai-2018-screen-paper-meta-analysis]] (g = .48, p = .11) — but note Kong's speed moderators are all self-disqualified at df < 4, and this paper measures only *easy* texts with a selected digital-preferring sample, so the two do not actually conflict. [[concepts/screen-vs-paper]] should say so rather than listing them as opposing results.
- The digital-preferring selection makes this a rare **preference-contingent** design: it directly addresses the confound that [[concepts/preference-versus-effectiveness]] identifies, and it is worth flagging as the only source that *selects* on preference rather than measuring it as an outcome.
- Its comprehension incoherence is a useful concrete example for [[concepts/methodology-critique]]: the same pattern (Z-driven significance, CI contradicting) is what makes this paper unusable as evidence for the shallowing hypothesis.
