# The Effect of Page Layout and Expectations on Visual Search

**Citation.** Tarling, K. A. (2009). *The effect of page layout and expectations on visual search: Do people adapt search strategy to make best use of page layout design?* MSc thesis (HCI with Ergonomics), University College London (supervisor: Duncan Brumby).

**Reading task(s).** Search (web/page scanning).

**Question/aim.** How people adapt visual search strategy to page-layout design; what features drive that adaptation.

**Method.** Eye-tracking experiment; layouts varied in **search-item density** and **number of items per group**.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Text/item density (sparse vs. dense); group size.

**Key findings.**
- Participants were sensitive to layout: gaze adapted **in favour of sparse text**, yet targets were located **faster in dense text** (fewer but longer visits).
- Less sensitivity to number of items per group; no learning effect of group size.
- Behavior fits a **rational, context-sensitive theory of search**.

**Effect direction & size.** Dense layout → faster target location despite preference for sparse; group-size effects weak.

**Limitations/caveats.** MSc thesis; specific layout set; preference vs. performance diverge (sparse preferred, dense faster).
