# The Influence of Reading Purpose on Inference Generation and Comprehension in Reading

**Citation.** Narvaez, D., van den Broek, P., & Ruiz, A. B. (1999). The Influence of Reading Purpose on Inference Generation and Comprehension in Reading. *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 91(3), 488–496.

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension (reading purpose manipulation).

**Question/aim.** Whether reading purpose (study vs. entertainment) changes the inferences readers generate and their comprehension.

**Method.** Behavioral + think-aloud. Participants read 2 texts aloud (online think-aloud) and 2 for comprehension (offline). Narrative vs. expository text types.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** None typographic — reading *purpose/goal* and text type (narrative vs. expository).

**Key findings.**
- Reading purpose did **not** affect offline comprehension scores but **did** affect online behavior (think-aloud).
- Study-purpose readers more often repeated text, acknowledged knowledge gaps, and evaluated content/writing than entertainment readers.
- Effect stronger for expository than narrative text. Inference generation is partly under strategic reader control.

**Effect direction & size.** Purpose → online inferential activity (yes), offline comprehension (no). Sizes not reported here.

**Limitations/caveats.** Think-aloud reactivity; small text set; offline null may reflect measure sensitivity.
