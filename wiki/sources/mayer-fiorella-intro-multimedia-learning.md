# Introduction to Multimedia Learning

**Citation.** Mayer, R. E., & Fiorella, L. Introduction to Multimedia Learning. In *The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning* (3rd ed.). Cambridge University Press.

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension/learning from words + pictures (theory anchor).

**Question/aim.** Define multimedia learning and lay out the research base and design principles for learning from words and pictures.

**Method.** Handbook introduction/theoretical overview (Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning, CTML).

**Style/layout variables (framework).** Text–graphic integration; signaling, spatial/temporal contiguity, coherence, redundancy — layout principles that govern combining words and images.

**Key findings / principles.**
- **Multimedia hypothesis:** people learn more deeply from words + pictures than words alone.
- Learning is constrained by **limited working-memory** channels (dual-channel, limited capacity, active processing) → design should manage cognitive load.
- Layout/signaling principles (coherence, signaling, contiguity) reduce extraneous load.
