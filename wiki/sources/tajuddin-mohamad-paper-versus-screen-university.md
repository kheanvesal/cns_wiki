# Tajuddin & Mohamad — paper versus screen at university (grey literature)

## Citation
Tajuddin, E. S., & Mohamad, F. S. (n.d.). *Paper versus Screen: Impact on Learning at the University*. Faculty of Cognitive Science & Human Development, University Malaysia Sarawak. **No journal, volume, or DOI. Undated manuscript.**

## Reading task(s)
**comprehension** and reading time (both intended).

## Question / aim
Determine whether reading format affects undergraduate students' reading comprehension and reading speed.

## Method
- **Design:** **Quasi-experimental**, two-group between-subjects comparison, non-randomized group assignment.
- **N:** **84** undergraduates, 42 paper / 42 screen.
- **Materials:** Printed short articles vs screen-displayed equivalents; multiple-choice comprehension items. A reading-speed training stage preceded testing. **Computer-use familiarity was neither measured nor controlled**; the mouse was deliberately withheld.
- **Measures:** BEHAVIORAL — comprehension score; a variable labelled "reading speed" that Table 2 shows is **time taken**. No eye-tracking, no self-report.

## Style/layout variables manipulated
Reading medium only. No font, spacing, or layout variables reported. **Text difficulty not reported.**

## Key findings
- **Comprehension did not differ by medium:** t(82) = −.37, **p = .72**, mean difference −.17, SE = .46, 95% CI [−1.07, .74].
- **The null is not merely null but trivial.** Table 1 gives paper M = 6.51 (SD 1.23) vs screen M = 6.60 (SD 0.82), a difference of 0.083; Table 3 gives 13.02 vs 13.19, a difference of 0.17. Against SDs of 0.82–2.45 this implies **|d| ≈ 0.03–0.07**.
- **Time taken differed significantly, favoring paper:** t(82) = −5.07, **p < .001** (printed ".00"), mean difference −5.20, SE = 1.02, CI [−7.21, −3.15]. Table 2: paper M = 12.27 (SD 2.35) vs screen M = 17.45 (SD 6.19).
- **The Results narrative directly contradicts the authors' own data**, claiming "participants scored significantly better for reading comprehension when they read using the screen."
- The stated explanation for that result cites Chen et al. (2014) and Sun et al. (2013) — i.e. a **statistically null** outcome is "explained" by appealing to other studies, with no reference to the paper's own p = .72.

## Effect direction & size if reported
Comprehension: **no reliable difference**, p = .72, |d| ≈ .03–.07. Time taken: paper less time, p < .001. As claimed by the authors: screen > paper comprehension — **unsupported by their own statistics.**

## Limitations/caveats
- **Not peer-reviewed and undated; no journal, volume, or DOI.** Grey literature with essentially no evidentiary weight; cite only as an example of reporting failure.
- **Multiple impossible/contradictory statistics:** Table 1 and Table 3 report different means (≈6.5/6.6 vs ≈13.0/13.2) for the *same* outcome; "Sig. = 0" and ".00" are impossible p-values; Levene's tests reject equal variance for both outcomes (F = 4.96, p = .03; F = 13.20, p = .00) yet the EVA rows are presented as valid; the second EVA block uses non-integer Welch df (71.56, 52.54) while still labeled "Equal Variance Assumed"; the significance footnote is truncated mid-token as `*p<.0`; the methods describe "an Independent T-Test **for paired samples**" despite an independent-group design; and a multicollinearity reference (Belsley, Kuh & Welsch, 1980) is misplaced inside the inferential-statistics description.
- **"Reading speed" is a mislabeled time measure**, so the direction of the effect is ambiguous as labeled.
- No speed-accuracy tradeoff analysis despite measuring both. Computer familiarity unmeasured.
- Discussion attributes results to CRT displays at 1000×800 (Gould, 1986) and a garbled "28.5% slower" citation chain (Muter et al. via Belmore, 1985) — both inapplicable to modern LCDs.

## Connections
- **Retained as a documented failure case, not as evidence.** Its headline claim — screen superior for comprehension — is refuted by its own p = .72, so it must never be cited as a counterexample to [[sources/mangen-walgermo-bronnick-2013-paper-screen-comprehension]] or [[sources/kong-seo-zhai-2018-screen-paper-meta-analysis]].
- Instructive because of **how** it fails: the Results prose asserts a result the tables do not support. This is the failure mode [[concepts/methodology-critique]] warns about, and it contrasts instructively with [[sources/kong-seo-zhai-2018-screen-paper-meta-analysis]], whose reporting errors (sign typo, non-integer df) are less consequential because the headline effects are internally consistent.
- Its unexplained-comprehension step **mis-cites [[sources/chen-et-al-2014-paper-screen-tablet-familiarity]]** to rationalize a null. Chen's actual result (paper > computer on shallow comprehension, p = .004) *supports* the paper-advantage position this study's authors were arguing against.
