# Effects of LLM Use and Note-Taking on Reading Comprehension and Memory

**Citation.** Kreijkes, P., Kewenig, V., Kuvalja, M., Lee, M., Hofman, J. M., Vitello, S., Sellen, A., Rintel, S., Goldstein, D. G., Rothschild, D., Tankelevitch, L., & Oates, T. (2026). Effects of LLM use and note-taking on reading comprehension and memory: A randomised experiment in secondary schools. *Computers & Education*, 243, 105514. (Cambridge Univ. Press & Assessment + Microsoft Research.)

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension + retention (learning), LLM-assisted reading.

**Question/aim.** How does using an LLM to study a text compare to traditional note-taking (or both) for comprehension and retention?

**Method.** Pre-registered RCT, within + between design; **405 students, aged 14–15**, England; two passages; comprehension + retention tested **3 days later**. Conditions: LLM alone vs note-taking alone vs both. Prompt-behaviour "archetypes" identified.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** None typographic — the manipulation is the **reading tool** (LLM vs note-taking), i.e. AI-mediated compression/explanation vs active encoding.

**Key findings.**
- **Note-taking (alone or with LLM) beat LLM-alone** on both retention and comprehension.
- **Students preferred the LLM** and rated it more helpful — a clean **preference ≠ performance** result.
- LLM valued for accessibility / reduced cognitive load; note-taking valued for deeper engagement + memory.
- Combining LLM + note-taking beats LLM alone.

**Effect direction & size.** Note-taking > LLM-alone (significant); see paper for magnitudes.

**Limitations/caveats.** Secondary-school sample; 3-day retention; specific LLM interface.
