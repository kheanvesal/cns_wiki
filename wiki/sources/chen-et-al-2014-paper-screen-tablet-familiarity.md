# Chen, Cheng, Chang, Zheng & Huang (2014) — paper vs screen vs tablet

## Citation
Chen, G., Cheng, W., Chang, T.-W., Zheng, X., & Huang, R. (2014). A comparison of reading comprehension across paper, computer screens, and tablets: Does tablet familiarity matter? *Journal of Computers in Education*, 1(2–3), 213–225. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40692-014-0012-z

## Reading task(s)
**comprehension** at two depth levels (primary).

## Question / aim
Compare comprehension across paper, tablet, and computer, and test whether **tablet familiarity** explains why tablets may fail to show the screen disadvantage reported for computers.

## Method
- **Design:** Between-groups experiment (3 media), one-way ANOVA for media plus one-way ANOVA within the tablet subgroup for familiarity levels. Tablet Familiarity Questionnaire (TFQ, based on Zheng et al. 2014), reliability **α = .916**; familiarity split high/medium/low by ±0.5 SD from the tablet group's mean.
- **N:** **90** college students, 30 per medium (paper 30, tablet 30, computer 30).
- **Materials:** Passages in three formats; tablet at 1,366×768 px. Resolution is **not reported for the paper or computer conditions**, so visual equivalence is unverified.
- **Measures:** BEHAVIORAL — shallow comprehension (multiple choice) and deep comprehension (summarization, scored by unspecified raters, **no inter-rater reliability reported**). SELF-REPORT — tablet familiarity (α = .916). No eye-tracking.

## Style/layout variables manipulated
Reading medium as a three-level factor; question type as shallow (MCQ) vs deep (summarization); device familiarity as a measured moderator.

## Key findings
- **Paper beat computer on shallow comprehension:** F(2,87) = **5.83, p = .004**; paper group M = **8.27, SD = 1.91**.
- **Paper vs tablet on shallow comprehension was NOT significant, p = .214** — the tablet condition is what nulls the three-way comparison.
- **Deep comprehension did not differ by medium at all:** F(2,87) = **1.81, p = .169**.
- **Tablet familiarity affected deep comprehension only.** Familiarity × MCQ (shallow): F = 0.15, **p = .865**, null. Familiarity × summarization (deep): F = **5.89, p = .008**. Within the tablet group, high-familiarity > low-familiarity on summarization (**p = .004**; high group M = **17.50, SD = 4.77**); high vs medium **p = .774** and medium vs low null.
- The authors conclude the medium effect is **confined to the shallow level**, and argue the medium/navigation mechanism rather than the reading-vs-test media switch drives it — explicitly claiming "the switch between the reading and test media was not the reason."

## Effect direction & size if reported
| Contrast | Outcome | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Paper vs computer | shallow | Paper > computer, F = 5.83, p = .004 |
| Paper vs tablet | shallow | Null, p = .214 |
| Any medium | deep | Null, F = 1.81, p = .169 |
| Familiarity | deep (tablet) | High > low, F = 5.89, p = .008 |
| Familiarity | shallow | Null, F = 0.15, p = .865 |

## Limitations/caveats
- **Misreported degrees of freedom for every familiarity analysis:** all four report df = (2, **87**), the full-sample denominator, applied to a subgroup of n = 30 where the correct denominator is (2, 27). Every familiarity p-value is unverifiable as printed.
- Cells of 30 with no reported power analysis. College students, despite motivation drawn from primary-school research.
- Between-groups with different texts per group. Two coarse comprehension measures only.
- Familiarity dichotomized by an arbitrary ±0.5 SD rule rather than validated tertiles.

## Connections
- **The cleanest within-study demonstration that "screen" is not one condition.** Paper > computer but not tablet is the empirical basis for treating screen type as a real moderator — which is exactly the moderator Kong found **non-significant** in the pooled data ([[sources/kong-seo-zhai-2018-screen-paper-meta-analysis]], b = .22, p = .41). This paper and the pooled null are in genuine tension: [[concepts/screen-vs-paper]] should carry this as an open contradiction rather than smoothing it.
- Its **deep-comprehension null (p = .169)** is the strongest single-study counterexample to a blanket screen-inferiority claim, and lines up with the shallow/deep distinction the wiki tracks in [[concepts/comprehension-levels]].
- Device familiarity as an actionable lever connects to [[concepts/expertise-familiarity]] — and note this is the paper other screen-vs-screen studies cite to explain their own nulls, including the mis-citation in [[sources/tajuddin-mohamad-paper-versus-screen-university]].
