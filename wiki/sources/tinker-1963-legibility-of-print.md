# Legibility of Print (Tinker, 1963)

**Citation.** Tinker, M. A. (1963). *Legibility of Print.* Iowa State University Press.

**Reading task(s).** All — foundational legibility reference cited across the corpus.

**Question/aim.** Consolidate decades of experimental research on what makes print legible and fast to read.

**Method.** Book-length synthesis of Tinker & Paterson's large experimental program (speed-of-reading, visibility, distance methods) plus others.

**Style/layout variables (reviewed).** Type size, typeface, line length, leading (line spacing), boldface, all-caps vs. lower case, color/contrast, printing surface.

**Key findings (classic baselines).**
- ~9–12 pt type with moderate line length and adequate leading is read fastest; extremes hurt.
- **All-capitals** text is read markedly slower than mixed case.
- Optimal line length is moderate; very long/short lines slow reading.
- Leading interacts with type size and line length.

**Effect direction & size.** Establishes the classic legibility optima later work refines/qualifies.

**Limitations/caveats.** 1963 print technology; pre-screen; speed/legibility focus (not deep comprehension). Screen-era work (Dyson) revisits many of these.
