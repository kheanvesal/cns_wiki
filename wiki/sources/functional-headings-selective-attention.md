# Functional Headings' Effects on Selective Attention and Reading Processes

**Citation.** *Functional Headings' Effects on Selective Attention and Reading Processes.* (Empirical study in the Lorch/Lemarié signaling tradition; full author/venue not captured in extracted text — see file `FunctionalHeadings_Final.pdf`.)

**Reading task(s).** Skimming/selective reading + comprehension (expository text).

**Question/aim.** How **functional headings** (headings conveying structural/demarcation rather than content information) affect selective attention and reading processes.

**Method.** Experimental text-processing study (expository texts) building on SARA signaling theory [[sources/lemarie-lorch-perywoodley-2012-headings]].

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Heading type (content vs. functional); whitespace/paragraph demarcation.

**Key findings.**
- Functional (non-content) headings still shape reading: they demarcate text objects and direct selective attention even without conveying topic meaning.
- Expository texts (vs. narrative) are where heading/structure signaling matters most, given structural variability.

**Effect direction & size.** Heading-type effects on attention allocation (see paper for magnitudes).

**Limitations/caveats.** Metadata incomplete from extraction; verify citation from original PDF. Expository-specific.
