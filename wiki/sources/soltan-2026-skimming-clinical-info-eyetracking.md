# Reading and Skimming Clinical Information: Insights from Experiments Examining Medical Students' Eye Movement Behaviour

**Citation.** Soltan, M. A., Warrington, K. L., Sidhu, K., Roney, A. G., McGowan, V. A., Moffatt, S., Paterson, K. B., Melville, C. R., & White, S. J. (2026). Reading and skimming clinical information: insights from experiments examining medical students' eye movement behaviour. *BMC Medical Education*, 26:76. doi:10.1186/s12909-025-08412-z.

**Reading task(s).** Skimming vs. reading for comprehension (clinical text).

**Question/aim.** How medical students read vs. skim clinical texts, and how text characteristics modulate this.

**Method.** Three eye-tracking experiments; each crosses reading task (comprehend vs. skim for gist) × a text characteristic (legibility, context, accuracy).

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Text **legibility** (plus context and accuracy manipulations).

**Key findings.**
- Skim-reading yields shorter reading times but poorer comprehension than careful reading (replicates the core tradeoff in a clinical domain).
- Legibility, context, and accuracy manipulations interact with the skim vs. comprehend task in shaping eye movements.
- Provides domain-specific insight for medical education/clinical reading.

**Effect direction & size.** Skim < comprehend on comprehension; text-characteristic × task interactions (see paper).

**Limitations/caveats.** Medical-student sample; clinical texts; multiple manipulations.
