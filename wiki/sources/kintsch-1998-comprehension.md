# Comprehension: A Paradigm for Cognition (Kintsch, 1998)

**Citation.** Kintsch, W. (1998). *Comprehension: A Paradigm for Cognition.* Cambridge University Press.

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension (foundational theory book, not a style study).

**Question/aim.** A general theory of comprehension — the Construction–Integration (CI) model — spanning discourse, memory, and problem solving.

**Method.** Theoretical monograph synthesizing decades of discourse-processing research; computational modeling (CI).

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** None. This is the theoretical *substrate* the corpus's comprehension claims rest on.

**Key findings / core constructs.**
- **Levels of representation:** surface form → **textbase** (propositional) → **situation model** (integrated with prior knowledge). Deep comprehension = a well-formed situation model.
- **Construction–Integration:** weakly-constrained construction of propositions, then constraint-satisfaction integration settles on a coherent interpretation.
- Comprehension is knowledge-driven; coherence, prior knowledge, and reading goal determine which representation level is reached.

**Effect direction & size.** N/A (theory).

**Limitations/caveats.** Predates most screen/digital reading work; needs bridging to typography/layout effects (which act mainly on surface processing and, indirectly, on resources for building the situation model).
