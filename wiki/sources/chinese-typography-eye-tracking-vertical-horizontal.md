# Chen, Yang & Wang (Scientific Reports 2024)

## Citation
Weilong Chen, Jiqiang Yang, and Yiluo Wang. 2024. "The influence of Chinese typography on information dissemination in graphic design: based on eye-tracking data." *Scientific Reports* 14: 13947. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-64964-y

## Reading task(s)
Comprehension (recall/recognition of poster content). Free-viewing with a 5-second exposure; closest to search-and-scan behavior within a poster rather than sustained prose reading, but the outcome measured is content recall.

## Question/aim
How does the arrangement of Chinese characters — vertical versus horizontal — affect attention allocation and information dissemination when combined with imagery in graphic design? The paper frames this as a design question for web pages and mobile app layouts, not as a reading-science question.

## Method
- **Design:** 2 × 2 between-subjects: layout (vertical vs. horizontal) × gender.
- **N:** 40 individuals recruited from a Chinese university, 20 per group (9 males, 11 females each group), ages 20–24 (`M=22.1`, `SD=1.4`), normal vision, fluent Chinese readers. Neither group had seen the materials before.
- **Materials:** two posters combining the text "雷锋的方向是我们的方向" ("Leifeng's direction is our direction") with images; font size, font type, and color were held constant across conditions by design.
- **Apparatus:** Tobii Pro Fusion eye tracker, 250 Hz, 60–65 cm viewing distance.
- **Procedure:** 5-second viewing window per poster (duration adopted from Ma & Chuang), followed by a two-item questionnaire: (1) multiple-choice recognition of three visual items, (2) free recall of the poster text.
- **Measures:** heatmaps, fixation duration on text region and image region, pupil diameter, gaze trajectory, number of fixations, and a 5-point recall score (1 point each for three recognition items; 0/1/2 for the text keywords 雷锋/方向).

## Key findings
- **Fixation duration on image:** significant main effect of text layout, `F(1,36)=11.926, p=0.001` — fixation duration on the image was significantly higher in the vertical-text condition. Significant layout × gender interaction, `F(1,36)=4.571, p=0.039`: in the vertical condition males fixated the image longer than females (`M=2174.60` vs. `M=1383.60`, `t(14.868)=−3.274, p=0.005`); no gender difference horizontally, `t(18)=0.659, p=0.518`.
- **Fixation duration on text:** significant main effect of layout, `F(1,36)=61.184, p<0.001`, with longer text fixation in the vertical condition. Layout × gender interaction, `F(1,36)=8.584, p=0.006`: vertically, females fixated text longer than males (`M=2487.10` vs. `M=1692.00`, `t(18)=2.607, p=0.018`); no gender difference horizontally, `t(18)=−1.365, p=0.189`.
- **Pupil diameter:** significant group differences, `p=0.024` for text and `p=0.001` for the image region, indicating greater engagement with graphics in the vertical group.
- **Fixation counts:** in the vertical group there was no significant difference between text and image fixation counts (`p=0.065`); horizontally, image fixations significantly exceeded text fixations (`p=0.036`).
- **Recall:** vertical group total 87 vs. horizontal group 70, `t(38)=3.569, p=0.001`.
- **Saccades:** the vertical group exhibited more saccades; horizontal subjects showed little cross-region revisiting but did re-view within a region.
- Authors' conclusion: vertical arrangement attracts attention and, combined with imagery, forms a visual "center of interest" that promotes meaning transfer; they recommend vertical as a primary format in media/web/mobile layout design.

## Effect direction & size if reported
**Vertical > horizontal** on text fixation duration (`F=61.184, p<.001`), image fixation duration (`F=11.926, p=.001`), pupil diameter (both regions), and recall (`t=3.569, p=.001`; 87 vs. 70 points). The recall gap is roughly 20% of the 5-point maximum scale. Effects are consistent in direction but moderated by gender, which the authors treat as a design-relevant interaction rather than a confound.

## Limitations/caveats
Very short exposure (5 s) and free viewing of posters — this is an attention/attractiveness study, not a reading-comprehension study. Single 8-character phrase; no narrative or sustained text. Sample is a narrow university student band (20–24) with no older, non-student, or dyslexic readers. Between-subjects layout assignment means no within-reader comparison. The authors explicitly note they controlled font size, font type, and color and studied layout only, so verticality effects cannot be separated from any residual interaction with image placement. Recall scoring was coarse (0–5). Reading direction is culturally specific to Chinese; the finding does not generalize to alphabetic scripts.
