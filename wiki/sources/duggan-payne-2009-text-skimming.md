# Text Skimming: The Process and Effectiveness of Foraging Through Text Under Time Pressure

**Citation.** Duggan, G. B., & Payne, S. J. (2009). Text skimming: The process and effectiveness of foraging through text under time pressure. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*, 15(3), 228–242. doi:10.1037/a0016995.

> **Duplicate note.** Same paper stored 3× in folder (`Duggan_Payne_JEPA_2009`, `Text_Skimming_The_Process_and_Effectiveness_of_For`, and its `(1)`). Ingested once.

**Reading task(s).** Skimming (under time pressure).

**Question/aim.** How effective is skimming, and what process do readers use when time-limited?

**Method.** Behavioral experiments (some eye-tracking) with expository text under varying time constraints; measured memory for main points vs. details.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Not typography — time pressure and text position/importance; readers allocate attention strategically.

**Key findings.**
- Skimming is **effective**: readers preferentially extract **main points** over details when time-limited.
- Readers **forage** — allocating reading time to high-value/important content, roughly optimally ("satisficing").
- Skimming preserves gist comprehension far better than random sampling of text would.

**Effect direction & size.** Main-point memory relatively preserved under time pressure; detail memory drops.

**Limitations/caveats.** Expository lab texts; time-pressure operationalization.
