# Type and Tale: The Effects of Typography and Narratives on Comprehension

**Citation.** Endres, T., Désiron, J. C., Vössing, C., Beck, L., Spiekermann, E., & Renkl, A. (2025). *Type and Tale: The Effects of Typography and Narratives on Comprehension.* (Preprint, submitted 18 Sep 2025.)

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension (typography + content features).

**Question/aim.** Whether typographic manipulations (e.g., disfluent fonts; sentence segmentation with indentation) and narrative framing affect comprehension/learning.

**Method.** Experimental; Bayesian ANOVAs (reports Bayes Factors BF01 supporting nulls); comprehension outcomes; controlled prior knowledge/interest across groups.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Typography — including meaningful-sentence segmentation paired with indentation, and disfluency-style manipulations; plus narrative vs. non-narrative content.

**Key findings.**
- Bayesian evidence **supports the null** for several demographic/prerequisite confounds (comparable groups).
- Meaningful sentence segmentation with indentation reduced [processing/time metric] relative to control (formatting that aligns with meaning helps).
- Content features (narratives) may exert effects comparable to typographic ones — content can matter as much as form.

**Effect direction & size.** Mixed; several null results (strong BF01); segmentation/indentation shows benefit. See preprint for exact BFs.

**Limitations/caveats.** Preprint (pre-peer-review); multiple manipulations; some nulls. Adds to the contested **disfluency → comprehension** debate.
