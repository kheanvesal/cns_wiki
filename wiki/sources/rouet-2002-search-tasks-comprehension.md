# Effects of Information Search Tasks on the Comprehension of Instructional Text

**Citation.** Rouet, J.-F., Vidal-Abarca, E., Bert-Erboul, A., & Millogo, V. (2002). Effects of information search tasks on the comprehension of instructional text. *Discourse Processes*.

**Reading task(s).** Search × Comprehension (the bridge between the two hubs).

**Question/aim.** How performing an information-search task shapes what readers comprehend and retain from instructional text.

**Method.** Experimental; readers process instructional text under search-task vs. read-for-comprehension conditions; comprehension/recall measures.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** None typographic — the manipulation is **task** (search vs. general comprehension); relevant text structure/organizers support locating task-relevant info.

**Key findings.**
- Search tasks induce **selective, task-relevance-driven** processing rather than uniform reading.
- Comprehension outcomes differ by task: search improves memory for targeted content but can reduce global understanding.
- Task goals steer which text information is deeply processed.

**Effect direction & size.** Task-dependent comprehension differences (see paper).

**Limitations/caveats.** Instructional text; lab task; classic pre-digital search framing.
