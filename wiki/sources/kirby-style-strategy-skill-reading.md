# Style, Strategy, and Skill in Reading (Kirby, Ch. 9)

**Citation.** Kirby, J. R. (1988). *Style, Strategy, and Skill in Reading.* Ch. 9 in *Learning Strategies and Learning Styles* (Springer, 978-1-4899-2118-5_9). (Year per `Readability Latent-Factor Matrix.xlsx`.)

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension/reading theory (filed under Proofreading in the corpus).

**Question/aim.** Distinguish reading **style/strategy** from **skill**, and argue their effects must be understood jointly.

**Method.** Theoretical chapter; framework + review of measurement problems.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** None — "style" here means *reader* processing style/strategy, not typographic style. Useful to disambiguate terminology in this wiki.

**Key findings.**
- Reading processes are a function of task difficulty, reader's general and specific skills, and reader's purpose.
- Style/strategy effects cannot be interpreted independently of skill; the two domains interact.
- A consensus reading theory can be outlined but not yet specified in prescriptive detail.

**Effect direction & size.** N/A (theory).

**Limitations/caveats.** Older chapter; conceptual. Note the **terminology clash**: "style" = cognitive strategy, not typography.
