# Fortune Favors the Bold (and the Italicized): Effects of Disfluency on Educational Outcomes

**Citation.** Diemand-Yauman, C., Oppenheimer, D. M. (& Vaughan, E. B.). Fortune Favors the Bold (and the Italicized): Effects of Disfluency on Educational Outcomes. *Proceedings of the Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society*, 32(32) (2010); journal version *Cognition* 118(1), 111–115 (2011).

**Reading task(s).** Comprehension/learning + retention (disfluency).

**Question/aim.** Whether **disfluent** (harder-to-read) fonts improve retention via "desirable difficulty."

**Method.** Lab study (varied fonts, incl. hard-to-read) + a field study in real classrooms; recall/retention outcomes.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Font disfluency (e.g., Comic Sans Italic / Monotype Corsiva / Haettenschweiler vs. clean fonts).

**Key findings.**
- Disfluent fonts **improved** retention in both lab and classroom (the classic "desirable difficulty" result).
- Effect attributed to deeper processing induced by perceptual difficulty.

**Effect direction & size.** Disfluent > fluent for retention (moderate). **Note: later replications frequently fail** — see contradictions.

**Limitations/caveats.** Original positive result; **poorly replicated** (esp. Sans Forgetica work). Treat as the *pro* side of the disfluency debate.
