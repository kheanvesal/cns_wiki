# Skim Reading by Satisficing: Evidence from Eye Tracking

**Citation.** Duggan, G. B., & Payne, S. J. (2011). Skim reading by satisficing: evidence from eye tracking. *CHI '11*, 1141–1150. doi:10.1145/1978942.1979114.

> **Duplicate note.** Same paper stored 2× (`DugganPayne11`, `Duggan_Payne_11`). Ingested once.

**Reading task(s).** Skimming (web, under time pressure).

**Question/aim.** What eye-movement strategy underlies effective skim reading of expository text on the Web.

**Method.** Eye-tracking; readers skim expository text under time pressure; analysis of which text gets fixated.

**Style/layout variables manipulated.** Text importance/position; time constraint (not typography).

**Key findings.**
- Skim reading operates by **satisficing**: readers read early/important sentences more, then move on once a "good enough" understanding is reached.
- Eye movements show selective allocation to informative regions, not uniform speed-up.
- Confirms the foraging account from [[sources/duggan-payne-2009-text-skimming]] with gaze data.

**Effect direction & size.** Selective fixation on high-value text; satisficing threshold governs skipping.

**Limitations/caveats.** Lab eye-tracking; expository web text.
