# Does Any Style Variable Improve Comprehension — Not Just Speed or Legibility?

**Short answer.** Yes — but only a specific *kind* of style variable. Variables that **scaffold text structure or manage cognitive load** (moderate line length, headings/signaling, structure-aligned organizers, good text–graphic integration) can genuinely improve comprehension. Variables that only change **surface legibility, aesthetics, or perceptual difficulty** (font size, typeface/serif-vs-sans, line/letter spacing, disfluency) mostly move *speed, comfort, or eye movements* — not comprehension.

This split follows directly from theory: in Kintsch's model ([[sources/kintsch-1998-comprehension]]), deep comprehension is limited by building a **[[concepts/situation-model]]**, not by decoding. Surface legibility only helps comprehension when text is hard enough that easing decoding **frees working-memory resources**; otherwise legibility gains cash out as speed, not understanding. Structural scaffolding, by contrast, acts on the level where comprehension is actually built.

## The organizing principle
- **Legibility ≠ comprehension.** Font size, typeface, and spacing sit at the surface level; they raise speed/comfort and shift eye movements ([[concepts/eye-tracking-measures]]) but leave the situation model largely unchanged once text is legible. See [[concepts/legibility]].
- **Structure/load variables reach the comprehension level.** Headings, organizers aligned to text structure, segmentation, and managed text–graphic integration scaffold macrostructure and reduce extraneous load — the operations that actually build understanding. See [[concepts/headings-signaling]], [[concepts/text-structure]], [[concepts/split-attention-cognitive-load]].
- **Moderators decide.** Benefits are largest for **hard text, lower-ability readers, expository genre, and deep (inferential/critical) comprehension** — precisely where resources are scarce ([[sources/fpsyg-12-712901]] shows deep levels are rarely reached) and where the reading **goal** demands depth ([[concepts/reading-purpose-and-goal]]).

## Caveats
- Most "helps" verdicts rest on **moderate** evidence (single studies, specific samples/genres); the "neutral" verdicts for size/typeface/spacing are comparatively **well-replicated nulls**.
- Many studies confound the variable with text type or measure only one axis — the matrix's **single-axis isolation** gap ([[concepts/latent-factor-matrix]]) means true style×structure interactions are under-tested.
- "Comprehension" is measured heterogeneously (literal vs. inferential; recall vs. questions) — [[concepts/question-type]] moderates apparent effects.

## Gen-AI update — a new content lever
Beyond typographic style, **LLMs now let you manipulate the content itself** — compression (summaries/outlines) and cohesion/level (personalized rewriting). Early evidence: AI summaries **help weak readers but harm strong ones** [[sources/rolle-2025-gpt-tools-comprehension]], and LLM-alone study underperforms note-taking for retention [[sources/kreijkes-2026-llm-notetaking-comprehension]]. This is consistent with the mechanism here — *over-easing* a text (too little effort) can hurt durable comprehension, the mirror image of disfluency's "desirable difficulty." Content transformation is the emerging frontier the style literature never reached; see [[syntheses/gen-ai-and-reading]].

## Bottom line for a practitioner
To help comprehension, invest in **structure and load management** (clear headings/signaling, structure-aligned layout, well-integrated graphics, moderate line length) rather than in **font choice or micro-spacing**, which mainly buy speed and comfort. Do not expect **disfluent fonts** to help.
