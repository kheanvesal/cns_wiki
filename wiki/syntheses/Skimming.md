# Skimming — Reading-Task Hub

How text style, layout, and typography affect **fast, selective reading for gist**. Core fact: skimming trades comprehension for speed, but not randomly — readers **satisfice/forage**, preferentially extracting main points and high-value content.

## How skimming works (the strategy)
- **Satisficing/foraging:** under time pressure readers allocate attention to important/early text and stop when "good enough," preserving gist over detail [[sources/duggan-payne-2009-text-skimming]] · [[sources/duggan-payne-2011-skim-satisficing]].
- **Adaptive, task-tuned:** skim depth is modulated to task; skimming lowers comprehension vs. careful reading but stays gist-effective [[sources/fitzsimmons-2014-skim-reading-web]] · [[sources/soltan-2026-skimming-clinical-info-eyetracking]].
- **Speed–accuracy tradeoff:** faster reading lowers comprehension, unevenly across question types (main-idea survives, detail drops) [[sources/dyson-haselgrove-2000-speed-patterns-screen]].
- **Reader strategies differ:** eye-fixation typologies for reading-to-summarize (linear vs. structure-processing readers) [[sources/hyona-lorch-kaakinen-2002-reading-to-summarize]]; goal/task produce global eye-movement adjustments [[sources/reading-goal-task-eye-fixation-patterns]].
- **Terminology:** skimming (gist) vs. scanning (specific info) vs. careful/expeditious reading [[sources/tamsi-skimming-scanning-teaching-narrative]] · [[sources/reading-in-a-second-language-book]].

## Layout & typography that support skimming
- **Headings/signaling** guide selective attention and macrostructure building — content vs. functional headings both matter [[sources/lemarie-lorch-perywoodley-2012-headings]] · [[sources/functional-headings-selective-attention]]. **★ Redirection, not recruitment:** [[sources/scaltritti-2019-typographic-variables-webpage-eye-movements]] found **more headers → fewer fixations (b = −0.92)**, and [[sources/lege-2019-typography-efl-eye-tracking]] found overall fixation quantity **unchanged** under typographic cueing while attention **redistributed** toward cued regions (AOI 6: 100% vs 62.07%, p = .0021). **⚠️ But Lege bundles 21 changes at once, so no individual cue is identified.** See [[concepts/signaling]].
- **Line length / cpl:** medium line length (~55 cpl) optimal; extremes hurt [[sources/dyson-haselgrove-2001-speed-linelength-screen]] · [[sources/layout-on-screen]].
- **Font size** eases eye-movement processing; **typeface (serif vs sans)** effects are weak/inconsistent [[sources/beymer-font-size-type-online-reading]] · [[sources/richardson-legibility-serif-sans-serif]] · [[sources/lund-1999-knowledge-construction-typography]].
- **Density:** dense layouts can locate targets faster despite sparse being preferred (see [[syntheses/Search]]).
- **Classic legibility optima** (size, leading, line length, avoid all-caps) [[sources/tinker-1963-legibility-of-print]]; craft norms [[sources/bringhurst-elements-of-typographic-style]].
- **Text+graphics / multimedia:** manage split-attention and cognitive load [[sources/mayer-fiorella-intro-multimedia-learning]].

## Medium & navigation
- **Screen < paper** for comprehension and metacognitive calibration, esp. under time pressure — a "shallowing"/inattention effect [[sources/clinton-2019-paper-vs-screens-meta]] · [[sources/delgado-salmeron-2021-inattentive-onscreen-reading]].
- **★ The metacognitive-calibration half of that claim is now unconfirmed here.** The pooled estimate holds for comprehension (**g = −.21, p = .02**, 17 studies) but **not** for speed (g = .48, p = .11) [[sources/kong-seo-zhai-2018-screen-paper-meta-analysis]]; no moderator reaches significance, and the only direct calibration test found screen metacomprehension **numerically higher but null** (p = .177, d = .21) [[sources/halamish-elbaz-2020-childrens-comprehension-metacomprehension]]. First graders showed **no medium effect at all** and **tablet > paper** on main-point questions [[sources/florit-2025-first-grade-comprehension-monitoring]]. Keep the shallowing account; do not restate calibration as settled. See [[concepts/screen-vs-paper]].
- **Scrolling vs. pagination** interacts with screen size [[sources/hemminger-marcial-2012-scrolling-pagination]]; multi-display active reading approximates paper [[sources/chen-multislate-active-reading]].
- **★ Speed and comprehension can point opposite ways across media.** [[sources/breso-grancha-2022-digital-print-easy-texts]]: print was **much slower** than digital (r = .91, p < .001) while the comprehension advantage sits with print. So "digital is faster" is not a safe default even within the paper-advantage literature. Note the source's impossible significance/CI combinations make it unusable as a quantitative citation — retained for the direction only. See [[concepts/methodology-critique]].

## Contradictions / open tensions
- **Disfluency debate (major):** "desirable difficulty" — disfluent fonts aid memory [[sources/diemand-yauman-oppenheimer-fortune-favors-bold]] — is **contradicted** by Sans Forgetica and other failures [[sources/dykes-sans-forgetica-readability]] · [[sources/font-disfluency-online-learning-highschool]] · [[sources/ssrn-5678368]]. Current weight: disfluency does **not** reliably help. See [[concepts/disfluency]].
- **★ Disfluency's cost is now demonstrated more often than its benefit, and the mechanism splits.** [[sources/janouskova-2022-font-readability-moses-illusion]]: the difficult font **lowered factual accuracy** (93% vs 77%, p = .039) while **leaving error detection unchanged** (43% vs 37%, p = .569, negligible by **equivalence testing** — the only such use in this corpus). If the benefit ran through deeper processing, error catching should have improved. Three of the four negative disfluency results now show the cost without the benefit, which is a stronger claim than "no benefit found."
- **★ "Skimming may be where disfluency hurts most" is now a testable prediction rather than a conjecture** — that line in this hub rested on the observation that slowing helps nobody. Janoušková shows a case where disfluency lowers accuracy while leaving a selective outcome alone, i.e. disfluency can penalize the processing that skimming depends on without buying anything. Still no study tests disfluency under a skim goal specifically.
- **Serif vs. sans:** long-assumed serif/print advantage is weak once confounds controlled [[sources/richardson-legibility-serif-sans-serif]] · [[sources/lund-1999-knowledge-construction-typography]] vs. older prescriptive claims [[sources/tinker-1963-legibility-of-print]].
- **★ Familiarity is not the answer.** [[sources/ho-sang-petrarca-2025-beyond-typeface-value]]: familiar faces were **preferred**, but were **not faster** and did **not** improve **recall**. This contradicts Tarasov's residual claim and removes the most attractive explanation for why people gravitate to familiar type. See [[concepts/expertise-familiarity]].
- **Screen penalty scope:** meta-analytic paper advantage [[sources/clinton-2019-paper-vs-screens-meta]] · [[sources/kong-seo-zhai-2018-screen-paper-meta-analysis]] is small and moderated by time pressure/task — not universal, and **not detectable in first graders**.
- **★ A grey-literature reversal that is refuted by its own data:** [[sources/tajuddin-mohamad-paper-versus-screen-university]] claims digital superiority, but is undated/un-peer-reviewed with **p = .72** in its own tables. Do not cite it.
- **Preference ≠ performance / method divergence** recurs throughout. See [[concepts/method-type-divergence]].
- **Machine-directed "skip what you know" can backfire.** Adaptive systems implicitly treat pruning already-mastered content as good skimming (see [[entities/bayesian-knowledge-tracing]]). Simulated evidence: adaptive reading helped computer science significantly, was inconclusive for inorganic chemistry, and was **neutral to slightly negative for general biology** [[sources/adaptive-personalization-educational-readings-simulated]] — i.e. the pruning rule failed when mastery estimates were wrong. Caveat: simulation, `n=3` domains, no humans. See [[concepts/adaptive-and-personalized-typography]] and [[concepts/simulated-readers]].
- **Removing visual line structure raises the cost of finding structure.** In prose layout (verse endings mid-line) readers lost their visual anchor and instead relied on rhyme to rebuild the poetic gestalt — rhyme anomalies there were *more* disruptive than in poem layout [[sources/rhythmic-subvocalization-poetry-eye-tracking]]. Reflowed text may carry an analogous cost. See [[concepts/layout-and-line-breaks]].
- **Context-adaptive typography improves throughput, not retention.** SituFont raised reading goodput and lowered subjective workload under simulated visual impairment while comprehension accuracy stayed flat [[sources/situfont-adaptive-mobile-typography-svi]] — a speed/understanding split consistent with the screen-shallowing pattern above.

## Open questions
- Does disfluency ever help under specific conditions, or is the effect essentially null? **★ Now sharpened:** the remaining live version is not "does disfluency help memory" but "is there any outcome where disfluency helps *without* costing speed and accuracy." Three sources now show cost without benefit.
- How much of the screen penalty is layout vs. mindset/inattention? **★ Note the constraint:** no study in the corpus separates scrolling from quantity of text, and Mangen's design had both media take the test on screen.
- Which signaling devices best support skimming without harming full-read comprehension? **★ Blocking issue:** the corpus's best structural evidence ([[sources/lege-2019-typography-efl-eye-tracking]]) bundles 21 cues and is EFL-only, so it cannot name a device.
- **When is adaptive pruning better than reading straight through?** The one corpus datapoint is negative in one of three domains and comes from simulation. See [[concepts/adaptive-and-personalized-typography]].
- **What does variable line breaking cost?** Nobody has tested deliberate line breaks as a skimming aid in prose, though the poetry evidence suggests structure-recovery costs rise when visual boundaries are removed.
- **Do per-reader optimized settings still produce good skimming?** All personalization evidence measures comprehension/accuracy on short items, never selective reading of long text.
- **★ Is the screen penalty absent for beginners or merely undetectable at that age?** Florit et al.'s null comes with tablet > paper on some questions — the opposite sign — and Kong's Year trend is non-significant (b = .26, p = .09). These cannot distinguish "no effect in children" from "effect exists but reverses." See [[concepts/screen-vs-paper]].
