# Skimming vs. Scanning vs. Search — How Layout Supports Differ

These three "fast reading" tasks are often lumped together but make **different demands on layout**. The distinction that organizes everything: **is the target known, and is it a specific item or a gist?**

- **Skimming** — read fast for **gist / main points**; no specific target. [[concepts/skimming-strategy]]
- **Scanning** — locate a **specific, known** item (a word, number, name). [[concepts/scanning]]
- **Search / information-seeking** — a broader goal-driven process, from directed lookup to exploratory browsing; the target may be ill-defined. [[concepts/browsing-vs-searching]]

Terminology anchors: expeditious vs. careful reading [[sources/reading-in-a-second-language-book]]; skim/scan as taught strategies [[sources/tamsi-skimming-scanning-teaching-narrative]]; search ≠ reading [[sources/fisher-1975-reading-visual-search]].

## What each task does with the eyes, and what layout helps

| | **Skimming** | **Scanning** | **Search / seeking** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Goal** | Extract gist / main points | Find a specific known item | Satisfy an info need (may be vague) |
| **Eye behavior** | Selective sampling; satisfice on high-value text | Rapid target-matching; peripheral guidance | Alternating browse ↔ directed fixation; navigation |
| **Best layout support** | **Signposting of importance**: clear headings/signaling, main points early/salient, moderate cpl | **Target discriminability & predictable location**: salience, consistent placement, adequate density | **Navigable structure**: good information structure, mode-appropriate navigation, relevance cues |
| **Density** | Neutral–secondary | **Dense can be faster** (though sparse preferred) | Interacts with screen size |
| **Key sources** | [[sources/duggan-payne-2009-text-skimming]], [[sources/duggan-payne-2011-skim-satisficing]], [[sources/lemarie-lorch-perywoodley-2012-headings]], [[sources/functional-headings-selective-attention]] | [[sources/fisher-1975-reading-visual-search]], [[sources/tarling-2009-page-layout-visual-search]], [[sources/zuo-2023-target-layout-graphic-search-eyetracking]] | [[sources/capra-marchionini-structure-interaction-search]], [[sources/choo-detlor-turnbull-1999-web-browsing-searching]], [[sources/thatcher-2006-cognitive-search-strategies]], [[sources/hemminger-marcial-2012-scrolling-pagination]] |

## Where they converge
- **All three benefit from clear structure/signposting** — headings and consistent layout help readers decide where to look, whether the target is a gist (skim), an item (scan), or a region (search).
- **All three are goal-driven and selective**, not linear reading — the reading goal produces a global adjustment ([[sources/reading-goal-task-eye-fixation-patterns]], [[concepts/reading-purpose-and-goal]]).

## Where they diverge (the useful part)
1. **Known vs. unknown target.** Scanning optimizes for **target discriminability** (make the item pop, place it predictably); skimming optimizes for **importance signposting** (mark the high-value regions, since there's no single target). Search sits between: often the "target" is a *region or answer*, so **navigable structure** matters most.
2. **Density.** Dense layouts speed **scanning/search target location** ([[sources/tarling-2009-page-layout-visual-search]]) even though readers prefer sparse — a [[concepts/method-type-divergence]] case. For **skimming**, density is secondary; signaling dominates.
3. **Navigation vs. within-page.** **Search** uniquely depends on cross-page navigation (scrolling vs. pagination × screen size, [[sources/hemminger-marcial-2012-scrolling-pagination]]; structure × interaction style, [[sources/capra-marchionini-structure-interaction-search]]). Skimming/scanning are mostly within-view.
4. **Familiarity.** Graphic/target familiarity neutralizes graphic-type differences in scanning/search ([[sources/zuo-2023-target-layout-graphic-search-eyetracking]]); skimming leans on content structure, less on symbol form.

## Cost to comprehension
All three trade depth for speed, but differently: skimming preserves **gist** at the cost of detail ([[concepts/skim-comprehension-tradeoff]]); scanning barely engages comprehension; a **search task reshapes comprehension** toward task-relevant content ([[sources/rouet-2002-search-tasks-comprehension]]).

## Gen-AI note
An **AI summary is machine-performed skimming** — compression done *for* the reader instead of *by* them. It removes the reader's own satisficing/foraging, which is why it can preserve gist yet cost durable comprehension, and disproportionately harm strong readers who would have skimmed effectively themselves [[sources/rolle-2025-gpt-tools-comprehension]], [[sources/kreijkes-2026-llm-notetaking-comprehension]]. Meanwhile the seek-vs-read distinction underlying "search" is now decodable from gaze [[sources/decoding-reading-goals-eye-movements-2024]]. See [[syntheses/gen-ai-and-reading]].

## Design takeaways
- Optimizing for **skimming** → invest in headings/signaling and front-load main points.
- Optimizing for **scanning** → maximize target salience and predictable placement; density is your friend.
- Optimizing for **search** → invest in information structure and navigation; support both browse and directed modes.
- If a page must serve all three, **clear structural signposting** is the common denominator; then tune density/navigation to the dominant task.

## Caveats
Much scanning/search evidence is from specific UIs/infographics and eye-tracking labs; "search" spans a huge range (lookup → exploratory). See the single-axis and interaction gaps in [[concepts/latent-factor-matrix]].
