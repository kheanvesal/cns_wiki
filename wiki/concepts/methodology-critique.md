# Methodology Critique (legibility research)

**What.** Meta-scientific critique that much legibility knowledge rests on weak/confounded methods.

## The reproducibility diagnosis
The corpus's two meta-scientific sources converge on **method, not style variables**, as the source of the field's contradictions.

- [[sources/lund-1999-knowledge-construction-typography]] — knowledge construction is undermined by typography chosen on aesthetic grounds.
- [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]] — far more explicit. Having compared ~a century of print legibility work, it detects "the similar problem in all of studies above. It is an absence of a unified approach… We suggest it is the principal reason of such contradictory results obtained."

**Named uncontrolled factors** (Tarasov): **lighting** (affects both color assessment and *qualimetric* perception, Daly 1993), **paper/substrate spectral properties**, and **absence of unified measurement units**. The last is the deepest — different labs measure "font size" in incompatible ways, so nominally comparable manipulations are not comparable.

**Proposed fix — a typographic measurement standard** (Tarasov; the corpus's only concrete protocol):
- **x-height in millimetres as the only measure of a typeface** (not point size).
- Line spacing expressed **as a fraction of x-height**, or in mm.
- Lighting per **ISO 3664:2009**; viewing distance **0.4 m**.
- Report illuminance, spectra, view angle, subjects' visual acuity, glasses.
- Fully specify stimuli: sheet size, column setting, line length, margins, x-height, mean inter-word / inter-letter / inter-line spacing, full typeface name, optical density of paper and text.

## Why this reframes the corpus
If the typeface and serif contradictions logged on [[syntheses/Comprehension]] are substantially **measurement artifacts rather than substantive disagreements**, then the practical consequence is that "which typeface is most legible" may be **unanswerable with the instruments the field has actually used** — not merely unsettled. The corpus never adopted x-height normalization, so its typeface results are not commensurable with each other. This is a stronger and more actionable claim than "evidence is mixed."

**Caveat.** Tarasov's own review is narrative, not systematic, and the standardization proposals are recommendations — never validated as an intervention. Treat the diagnosis as well-motivated, not proven.
