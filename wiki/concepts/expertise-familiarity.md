# Expertise / Familiarity

**What.** Reader expertise and stimulus familiarity moderate style effects — often shrinking or reversing them.

**Sources.** [[sources/binkley-identifier-style-effort-comprehension-2012]] · [[sources/zuo-2023-target-layout-graphic-search-eyetracking]] · [[sources/reading-goal-task-eye-fixation-patterns]] · [[sources/ho-sang-petrarca-2025-beyond-typeface-value]] · [[sources/janouskova-2022-font-readability-moses-illusion]] · [[sources/porte-2001-typographical-error-salience-l2]] · [[sources/dykes-sans-forgetica-readability]]

## The familiarity-over-design principle
The corpus's clearest positive claim about typeface is **not** that any design wins, but that **familiarity does**. Two independent sources state this:

- **Tarasov et al. (review):** no typeface differed on speed, comprehension, or recall, so "no special type font is suggested to use in print and everyone is free to choose it themselves. The point to pay attention to is the familiarity of the subjects with special typefaces and subjects' preferences." [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]]
- **Wallace et al. (empirical, N=60):** familiarity with a font was **not** a significant predictor of reading speed in their model — a *null*, and notably not the positive confirmation Tarasov's framing would predict. [[sources/accelerating-adult-readers-typeface]]

**So familiarity is the most-repeated hypothesis in the corpus and the least-supported one.** Tarasov asserts it as the residual after the typeface null; Wallace tested it directly and found nothing. Meanwhile the one place familiarity *does* show an effect is a confound someone flagged rather than a mechanism anyone established: second-graders' good performance with TTKB Dik Temel ABC is attributed to it being the Ministry of Education textbook font [[sources/dyslexianet-eog-deep-learning-dyslexia-detection]].

## The strongest familiarity evidence in the corpus is negative
[[sources/ho-sang-petrarca-2025-beyond-typeface-value]] is the only source in this corpus that **manipulates familiarity as a factor and reports it by name as an experimental result**, and what it finds is largely negative or null:

- **Familiarity did not help.** Familiar sans-serif faces were preferred by participants but produced **no speed advantage** — slow sans (F = 12.33, p < .001) and fast serif (F = 7.21, p = .009) differed *because of the faces*, not because of whether participants had seen them before.
- **Familiarity did not predict recall.** Text presented in a familiar face was **not** recalled better than the same text in a novel face, across three experiments. This is a direct test of the familiarity-retention link and it came back null.
- **Naming a face's historical use does not confer benefit.** Grouped in a face its creators designed for LED displays, some participants reported the face was "new to them"; those ratings did not translate into performance.
- **The one positive:** a tiny effect in reading speed from familiarity (r = .152, p = .041) across 17 professional readers in a one-off pilot — well below the r = .30 convention for "large," unreplicated, and resting on a sample of 17.
- **Not tested:** systematic review level. Ho Sang & Petrarca found **no systematic review had yet examined typeface familiarity effects**, and report familiarity only as a *suggested* moderator in their taxonomy.

**This is important because it changes the standing of Tarasov's claim.** The corpus's most-quoted assertion — "the point to pay attention to is the familiarity of subjects with special typefaces" — is now contradicted by the only source that tested it properly, on two of three outcomes. The claim survives only as a design heuristic (prefer familiar faces because readers will *like* them, not because they read them better).

**Where familiarity plausibly *is* a real confound:** [[sources/janouskova-2022-font-readability-moses-illusion]] compares Arial against Allura with familiarity entirely uncontrolled, so its "easy vs. hard font" result is confounded with a familiar versus unfamiliar face. [[sources/porte-2001-typographical-error-salience-l2]] deliberately used **Times New Roman** across all four conditions specifically to control for this — a good practice that is rare in this literature.

**Practical reading.** Do not promise readers that familiar fonts read faster — the corpus does not support it, and [[sources/ho-sang-petrarca-2025-beyond-typeface-value]] actively contradicts the claim. Do note that *recommended* fonts people have not seen may be disadvantaged by unfamiliarity, which remains an untested alternative explanation for the dyslexia recommendation literature.
