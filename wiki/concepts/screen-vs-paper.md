# Screen vs. Paper (Reading Medium)

**What it is.** The effect of presentation **medium** — digital screen vs. printed paper — on reading performance, time, and metacognition.

**Scope note.** This is the corpus's largest evidence cluster and the one place where a pooled quantitative estimate exists. It is *not* a style-variable question in the usual sense — medium bundles screen type, scrolling, text length, and device familiarity. Several studies below hold those constant; most do not.

## The pooled estimate

**Kong, Seo & Zhai (2018)** [[sources/kong-seo-zhai-2018-screen-paper-meta-analysis]] — 17 studies, 3,323 participants' worth of literature:
- **Comprehension favors paper:** screen−paper **g = −.21, 95% CI [−.38, −.03], p = .02**, I² = 73.23%.
- **Speed does not differ:** g = .48, 95% CI [−.15, 1.11], p = .11, I² = 93.36%.
- **No moderator reached significance** for either outcome — not year, not country, not screen type. Gender, grade, sample size, design, and sampling method were all excluded as unusable. So the meta-analysis cannot say *when* or *for whom* the penalty applies.

This corroborates the earlier pooled estimate in [[sources/clinton-2019-paper-vs-screens-meta]].

## The one dissociation the whole cluster agrees on

**Comprehension changes; speed does not.** Independently reproduced at the primary-study level in [[sources/halamish-elbaz-2020-childrens-comprehension-metacomprehension]] (comprehension d = .41, p = .036; speed p = .729) and stated as a law of the field in [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]] ("reading speed is more sensitive to typographical factors rather than to comprehension and recall"). Neither speed nor time is the thing medium reliably damages.

**Caveat on the speed evidence:** the largest speed effect in the cluster is [[sources/breso-grancha-2022-digital-print-easy-texts]] (print much *slower*, r = .91, p < .001) — the opposite direction from the pooled point estimate. It does not contradict Kong, because Kong's speed set is 8 studies / 19 effect sizes at I² = 93%, and **Kong's own Table 5b disqualifies every speed moderator** (df < 4, "should not be trusted" by the paper's own note). Treat "digital is faster" as unestablished rather than settled.

## Where the evidence genuinely disagrees

### 1. Age — the effect may not exist in beginner readers
[[sources/florit-2025-first-grade-comprehension-monitoring]] (N = 58 first graders, mean age 6.8) found **no medium main effect at all** on main-point, literal, or inferential comprehension (all ps > .15), with constant font size and short self-paced texts. Kong's Year trend (b = .26, p = .09) is *directionally* consistent with a shrinking penalty but **is not statistically significant and must never be cited as evidence of change over time.** Note also that Florit found **tablet > paper** for main-point questions on descriptive texts — the opposite of the pooled direction.

### 2. Screen type — pooled null, but strong within-study split
Kong's screen-type moderator is null (b = .22, CI [−.42, .86], p = .41). Yet [[sources/chen-et-al-2014-paper-screen-tablet-familiarity]] found, *within one study*, that **paper beat computer (p = .004) but not tablet (p = .214)**. Averaging these is exactly what a null moderator does. **This contradiction should stay open** — it means "screen" is not one condition, and the pooled estimate is an average over conditions that behave differently.

### 3. Comprehension depth — the effect may be shallow-only
Chen et al. found the medium effect on **shallow** comprehension (multiple choice) but **no medium effect on deep comprehension** (summarization, F = 1.81, p = .169). Device familiarity affected *deep* comprehension only (F = 5.89, p = .008). This is the corpus's only depth-resolved test of medium, and it argues against a blanket screen-inferiority claim. See [[concepts/comprehension-levels]].

### 4. Genre — no interaction in adolescents, small one in first graders
[[sources/mangen-walgermo-bronnick-2013-paper-screen-comprehension]] found **no genre × medium interaction** (p = .707) — the prediction that screen would hurt expository text more than narrative was not supported. Florit et al. found a **small** interaction on main-point and literal questions only (η² = .02–.04), null for inferential (p = .69). Same nominal variable, opposite-ish results, different ages. Open.

### 5. Metacognitive calibration — the wiki's prior claim is now contested
The pre-existing position on this page, from [[sources/clinton-2019-paper-vs-screens-meta]], was that **metacognitive calibration is worse on screen**. [[sources/halamish-elbaz-2020-childrens-comprehension-metacomprehension]] found metacomprehension judgments **numerically higher on screen but not significant** (t(37) = 1.38, p = .177, d = .21). **This does not support the prior claim, but it also does not refute it** — a null in N = 38 with a small effect is weak evidence either way. Meanwhile Florit et al. found comprehension monitoring predicted comprehension **regardless of medium**. Taken together: **monitoring tracks comprehension, and no study here shows it tracking medium.** The honest position is that the calibration claim is unconfirmed in this corpus, not that it has been overturned. See [[concepts/metacognitive-calibration]].

## Mechanism candidates — none confirmed here
- **Shallowing / inattentive reading:** on-screen readers mind-wander more and fail to adapt under time pressure [[sources/delgado-salmeron-2021-inattentive-onscreen-reading]]. **But** if screen reading degraded comprehension through shallower processing, metacomprehension should have shifted with it — it did not (p = .177). So the mechanism predicts an effect the calibration data does not show.
- **Navigation / spatial instability:** Mangen's own explanation. Scrolling was permitted and unmeasured; they concede they have no data on it.
- **Reading-for-comprehension as the antagonist process** (West 1983), invoked in [[sources/porte-2001-typographical-error-salience-l2]] for proofreading.

## What this cluster does *not* establish
- **Speed.** Null pooled; one large opposite-direction study; all moderators self-disqualified.
- **When or for whom.** No moderator in the meta-analysis reaches significance, and the moderators most relevant to the disputes (grade, sample size, design) were excluded as unusable.
- **Scrolling vs. quantity.** No source separates them — see the overload confound in [[sources/lu-et-al-2011-visual-search-information-overload]].

## Study-quality notes
[[sources/tajuddin-mohamad-paper-versus-screen-university]] appears to contradict Mangen (claiming screen comprehension superiority) but is **grey literature, undated, un-peer-reviewed**, and its own tables give p = .72 for comprehension. **It is refuted by its own data and must not be cited as counter-evidence.** Retained as a documented reporting failure; see [[concepts/methodology-critique]].

[[sources/mangen-walgermo-bronnick-2013-paper-screen-comprehension]] has a design confound worth stating plainly: **both groups took the comprehension test on screen**, so only the encoding medium varied. [[sources/halamish-elbaz-2020-childrens-comprehension-metacomprehension]]'s positive result sits **exactly at its own power floor** (d = .41 observed; two-tailed detectable minimum d ≥ .47). Neither is as strong as its effect size suggests.

## Evidence gaps worth chasing
1. **A screen-type moderator that works.** Kong's null may be an artifact of averaging computer and portable devices. Chen et al. show the split exists within a single study.
2. **Longitudinal trend with adequate power.** The "diminishing penalty" hypothesis currently rests on one non-significant coefficient. A pre-registered re-analysis or a new meta-analysis with year as a pre-specified moderator would settle it.
3. **Separating scrolling from quantity.** The medium manipulation is almost always confounded with scrolling; no source in the corpus breaks this.
4. **Metacognitive calibration, adequately powered.** The corpus's only direct test is underpowered and null.
5. **A comprehension test on a medium independent of the reading medium.** Mangen's core confound has been present in this literature since 2013 and has not been designed out.
