# Screen vs. Paper (Reading Medium)

**What it is.** The effect of presentation **medium** — digital screen vs. printed paper — on reading performance, time, and metacognition.

**Evidence.**
- Meta-analysis: small but reliable **paper advantage** for comprehension, esp. informational texts; **metacognitive calibration** worse on screen; reading-time differences inconsistent [[sources/clinton-2019-paper-vs-screens-meta]].
- Mechanism: on-screen readers **mind-wander more** and fail to adapt under time pressure ("inattentive"/shallowing) [[sources/delgado-salmeron-2021-inattentive-onscreen-reading]].
- Proofreading medium effects, moderated by task/text [[sources/vitello-2022-screen-vs-paper]].
- Design responses: multi-slate/active-reading systems try to recover paper's flexibility [[sources/chen-multislate-active-reading]]; scrolling/pagination × screen size [[sources/hemminger-marcial-2012-scrolling-pagination]].
- Typeface class (serif/sans) matters **less** than the medium framing implies [[sources/richardson-legibility-serif-sans-serif]].

**Nuance / contradiction.** The paper advantage is **small and moderated** (time pressure, text type, genre) — not universal. Much of it may be mindset/attention rather than optics.
