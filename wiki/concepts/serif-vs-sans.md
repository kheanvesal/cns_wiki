# Serif vs. Sans Serif

**What it is.** The long-running question of whether serif typefaces are more legible (esp. in print) and sans serif better on screen.

**Evidence.** The assumed advantages are **not robustly supported**:
- Differences are small and confounded by size/spacing/exposure once controlled [[sources/richardson-legibility-serif-sans-serif]].
- Methodological critique: much serif-superiority "knowledge" rests on weak methods [[sources/lund-1999-knowledge-construction-typography]].
- Online gaze effects of type are weak [[sources/beymer-font-size-type-online-reading]].
- Older prescriptive claims (e.g., all-caps slower; type norms) predate these controls [[sources/tinker-1963-legibility-of-print]].

**Bottom line.** No dependable general legibility advantage for serif or sans; choose for context/branding, not measured reading gains.
