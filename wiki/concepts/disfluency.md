# Disfluency (Desirable Difficulty)

**What it is.** Making text perceptually *harder* to read (hard-to-read fonts, low contrast, italics) on the hypothesis that the extra processing effort deepens encoding and improves memory — a "desirable difficulty."

**Operationalization across studies.** Hard fonts (Monotype Corsiva, Haettenschweiler, Comic Sans italic), the purpose-built **Sans Forgetica**, meaningful segmentation/indentation, low contrast.

**Evidence — contested (this is a headline contradiction in the corpus).**
- **Pro:** [[sources/diemand-yauman-oppenheimer-fortune-favors-bold]] found disfluent fonts improved retention in lab + classroom.
- **Con / null:** [[sources/dykes-sans-forgetica-readability]] (Sans Forgetica fails; may not even be disfluent); [[sources/font-disfluency-online-learning-highschool]] (slows reading, no reliable retention gain); [[sources/ssrn-5678368]] (Bayesian nulls for typographic manipulations).

**Current weight of evidence.** Disfluency does **not** reliably improve learning/retention; the original positive effect replicates poorly. Costs (slower reading) are more robust than benefits.

**Task interactions.** Any slowing may hurt **skimming** most; claimed benefits were for deep retention, which is where replications fail.
