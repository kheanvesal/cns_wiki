# Disfluency (Desirable Difficulty)

**What it is.** Making text perceptually *harder* to read (hard-to-read fonts, low contrast, italics) on the hypothesis that the extra processing effort deepens encoding and improves memory — a "desirable difficulty."

**Operationalization across studies.** Hard fonts (Monotype Corsiva, Haettenschweiler, Comic Sans italic), the purpose-built **Sans Forgetica**, meaningful segmentation/indentation, low contrast, and — outside this corpus but relevant — script faces such as Allura [[sources/janouskova-2022-font-readability-moses-illusion]].

## The mechanistic problem: disfluency hurts performance without producing the reflective state
The corpus's live hypothesis is that disfluency works by *forcing deeper processing*, which should show up as better **retention**. [[sources/janouskova-2022-font-readability-moses-illusion]] is the source that shows what happens when the reflective outcome is measured directly, and the result is a dissociation:

- Difficult font → **worse factual accuracy** on the undistorted question (93% Arial vs 77% Allura, z = 2.06, p = .039). **The manipulation demonstrably worked.**
- Difficult font → **no improvement in error detection**, the very behavior the Song & Schwarz illusion account predicts should benefit (43% vs 37%, z = −0.57, p = .569), with **equivalence testing** establishing the effect as negligible against a pre-specified smallest-effect threshold.

**This is not simply another null — it splits the account.** If disfluency's benefit ran through deeper processing, harder-to-read text should have improved error catching here. It did not, while the same font demonstrably degraded comprehension. So in this paradigm disfluency **costs performance without buying reflection**. Note also that this is a **failed replication of the original Song & Schwarz result**, in the paradigm where that result was obtained.

**Evidence — contested (this is a headline contradiction in the corpus).**
- **Pro:** [[sources/diemand-yauman-oppenheimer-fortune-favors-bold]] found disfluent fonts improved retention in lab + classroom.
- **Con / null:** [[sources/dykes-sans-forgetica-readability]] (Sans Forgetica fails; may not even be disfluent); [[sources/font-disfluency-online-learning-highschool]] (slows reading, no reliable retention gain); [[sources/ssrn-5678368]] (Bayesian nulls for typographic manipulations).

**Current weight of evidence.** Disfluency does **not** reliably improve learning/retention; the original positive effect replicates poorly. Costs (slower reading, lower accuracy) are more robust than benefits. **Three of the four negative results now include at least one that shows the cost without the benefit**, which is a stronger statement than "no benefit found": the interference side of the trade is demonstrated; the benefit side is not.

**Task interactions.** Any slowing may hurt **skimming** most; claimed benefits were for deep retention, which is where replications fail. The Janoušková dissociation adds a third case: **error detection**, where disfluency left detection untouched while lowering accuracy — so the harm can land on a task that has no retention story to protect it.

**Confound to keep naming.** Disfluency manipulations almost always vary **legibility and familiarity together**. Sans Forgetica "may not even be disfluent" [[sources/dykes-sans-forgetica-readability]], and Janoušková's Arial-vs-Allura comparison confounds hard-to-read with unfamiliar-face. Until a study varies legibility within a familiar face family, "disfluency" and "unfamiliarity" remain entangled in this literature.
