# Font Size

**What it is.** The physical size of type. **How you measure it matters as much as the value** — see the measurement standard below.

**Evidence.**
- Larger size aids **speed/legibility**: 12pt read faster than 10pt (EFL print) — but **no** effect on comprehension/recall [[sources/ej1079769]].
- Size affects **eye-movement** behavior online (fixation duration/number) [[sources/beymer-font-size-type-online-reading]].
- Classic optima ~9–12pt read fastest; extremes hurt [[sources/tinker-1963-legibility-of-print]].
- **The century-scale review finds preferences "highly dispersed," with a mean most-legible size near 12 points "but without specification of typeface"** [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]]. So ~12pt is the one numeric convergence in this corpus — but with the typeface left unspecified, which is precisely the problem below.
- The old claim is **sensitivity-limited, not settled**: Tinker's account says larger sizes mean peripheral vision perceives fewer words and reading speed *decreases*, yet "many of subsequent studies have found no significant differences between different font sizes" [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]].
- **A group-level signal where individual ones failed:** dyslexia status predicted font size (`Somers' D=0.273, p=.013`) even though it did *not* predict spacing or typeface [[sources/validating-personalized-visual-auditory-parameters-dyslexia]] — atypical readers dispersed across larger sizes.
- Print size specifically in dyslexia: O'Brien, Mansfield & Legge (2005), *the effect of print size on reading speed in dyslexia*, cited in [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]]; not independently held in this corpus.

## ⚠️ Measurement standard (why font-size results don't replicate)
Point size is **not** a comparable unit across typefaces — the same point size yields different x-heights, and x-height is what determines apparent size. Tarasov et al. propose **x-height in millimetres as the only measure of a typeface**, line spacing expressed as a fraction of x-height, lighting per ISO 3664:2009, and viewing distance fixed at 0.4 m [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]]. No study in this corpus followed it. The corpus's font-size findings are therefore **not commensurable with one another**, and the agreement around ~12pt may be partly an artifact of measuring in the wrong unit.

**Pattern.** Size helps legibility/speed and eases eye movements; it does **not** by itself raise comprehension once text is legible. Interacts with line length and spacing.
