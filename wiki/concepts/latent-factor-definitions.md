# Latent-Factor Definitions

Reference glossary for the 12 factors used in [[syntheses/reading-goal-x-controlling-latent-factors]]. Each entry: **what it is · how it's operationalized · a corpus + illustrative example.** Grouped by the [[concepts/text-style-vs-layout]] distinction. Validity caveats: [[concepts/latent-factor-matrix-critique]].

## Visual factors (V)

### V1 — Spatial density  *(layout; partly bridge)*
**What.** How much text/ink sits per unit of area — the openness vs. crampedness of the setting.
**Operationalized as.** Line spacing (leading), letter spacing, line length / [[concepts/characters-per-line]], margins/whitespace. → [[concepts/line-spacing]] · [[concepts/letter-spacing]] · [[concepts/line-length]] · [[concepts/whitespace-margins]].
**Example.** Corpus: single vs double leading and 10 vs 12 pt in EFL print [[sources/ej1079769]]; page margins on a handheld screen [[sources/2534-handheld-whitespace-reading]]. Illustrative: a double-spaced page with wide margins (low density) vs a cramped justified block (high density).

### V2 — Glyph legibility  *(text style)*
**What.** How easily individual letterforms are recognized.
**Operationalized as.** Typeface, font size, weight, letter case, disfluency. → [[concepts/font-type-typeface]] · [[concepts/font-size]] · [[concepts/serif-vs-sans]] · [[concepts/letter-case]] · [[concepts/disfluency]].
**Example.** Corpus: Arial vs Bookman Old Style at 10 vs 12 pt [[sources/ej1079769]]; the disfluent font Sans Forgetica [[sources/dykes-sans-forgetica-readability]]. Illustrative: clean sans at 12 pt vs a hard-to-read condensed italic.

### V3 — Salience distribution  *(layout; emphasis is a bridge)*
**What.** How visual emphasis and attention are allocated across the page — what "pops."
**Operationalized as.** Bold/italic emphasis, colour, cueing, attention guidance. → [[concepts/emphasis-bold-italic]] · [[concepts/signaling]] · [[concepts/selective-attention]].
**Example.** Corpus: bold/italic markup as a difficulty feature of web pages [[sources/martin-calpena-web-comprehension-2022]]; attention cueing between text and an embedded visualization [[sources/3320435-3320447]]. Illustrative: bolding the one key term in a paragraph so the eye lands there first.

### V4 — Temporal presentation  *(layout / delivery)*
**What.** The *time* dimension of delivery — pacing and how fast content arrives. (Per [[concepts/latent-factor-matrix-critique]], the xlsx mis-coded screen-vs-paper here; true V4 = pacing, not medium.)
**Operationalized as.** Reading speed, self- vs externally-paced (RSVP), time pressure. → [[concepts/reading-speed]] · [[concepts/time-pressure]] · [[concepts/scrolling-vs-paging]].
**Example.** Corpus: normal vs fast reading speed on screen [[sources/dyson-haselgrove-2000-speed-patterns-screen]]; time pressure that triggers satisficing [[sources/duggan-payne-2009-text-skimming]]. Illustrative: RSVP flashing one word at a time at a fixed rate (pure temporal control).

### V5 — Layout-structure mapping  *(layout)*
**What.** How well the *visual* layout signals the text's underlying structure.
**Operationalized as.** Headings, indentation, graphic organizers, structural cues aligned to content. → [[concepts/headings-signaling]] · [[concepts/text-structure]] · [[concepts/graphic-organizers]] · [[concepts/segmentation-indentation]].
**Example.** Corpus: headings as signalling devices [[sources/lemarie-lorch-perywoodley-2012-headings]]; T-charts vs Venn diagrams aligned to text structure [[sources/ej1486502]]. Illustrative: a heading plus indentation that mirrors the argument's parts.
*(Merged with L5 as one "text-structure" construct in the matrix — r=+0.66.)*

### V6 — Navigation structure  *(layout)*
**What.** How the reader moves through the content.
**Operationalized as.** Linear scroll ↔ paginated ↔ hyperlinked ↔ tiered/collapsible; links per page. → [[concepts/scrolling-vs-paging]] · [[concepts/interaction-style]] · [[concepts/information-structure]].
**Example.** Corpus: scrolling vs pagination × screen size [[sources/hemminger-marcial-2012-scrolling-pagination]]; links-per-page as a web feature [[sources/martin-calpena-web-comprehension-2022]]. Illustrative: a collapsible outline vs one long continuous scroll.

## Linguistic factors (L)

### L1 — Lexical sophistication  *(text-side)*
**What.** Vocabulary difficulty — word rarity and technicality.
**Operationalized as.** Word frequency, technical terms, morphological complexity. → [[concepts/cognitive-strategies]] (context).
**Example.** Corpus: technical-vocabulary problems in scientific writing [[sources/hyatt-et-al-2017-proofreading-writing-science]]; compound-identifier recognition in code [[sources/binkley-identifier-style-effort-comprehension-2012]] (note: the critique reclassifies this as orthographic segmentation, not vocabulary). Illustrative: "use" vs "utilize"; everyday vs specialist terms.

### L2 — Syntactic complexity  *(text-side; never manipulated — gap)*
**What.** Sentence-structure difficulty.
**Operationalized as.** Clause embedding, sentence length, active vs passive voice.
**Example.** Corpus: sentence-clarity issues flagged in student writing [[sources/hyatt-et-al-2017-proofreading-writing-science]] — but **no study varies it as an IV** (a top gap). Illustrative: "The cat sat." vs "The cat that the dog that the child owned chased sat."

### L3 — Cohesion  *(text-side)*
**What.** How explicitly ideas connect — the glue that lets a reader build a [[concepts/situation-model]].
**Operationalized as.** Connectives, referential coherence, given-new ordering, bridging inferences. → [[concepts/situation-model]] · [[concepts/textbase]] · [[concepts/inference-generation]].
**Example.** Corpus: the coherence machinery of Kintsch's CI model [[sources/kintsch-1998-comprehension]]; inference bridging under different reading purposes [[sources/journal-of-educational-psychology-1999]]. Illustrative: adding "because" and "as a result" to link otherwise-bare sentences.

### L4 — Compression  *(text-side; only ever an outcome — gap)*
**What.** How condensed the text is relative to full content.
**Operationalized as.** The continuum full text → summary → TL;DR → headline. → [[concepts/main-points-vs-detail]].
**Example.** Corpus: expeditious/skim reading treated as a *compressed reading type* [[sources/duggan-payne-2009-text-skimming]] — compression appeared only as an **outcome**, never a manipulated input in the 63-paper corpus. *Gen-AI update:* LLM summaries/outlines now make compression a manipulable **input** with real, ability-dependent effects [[sources/rolle-2025-gpt-tools-comprehension]], [[sources/kreijkes-2026-llm-notetaking-comprehension]] ([[syntheses/gen-ai-and-reading]]). Illustrative: the same story as article → abstract → one-line headline.

### L5 — Rhetorical scaffolding  *(text-side; bridges to V5)*
**What.** Explicit *verbal* signposting of the argument's organization (the linguistic counterpart of V5).
**Operationalized as.** Topic sentences, advance organizers, "first/second/finally" signposts, macro-structure markers. → [[concepts/text-structure]] · [[concepts/reading-strategies]].
**Example.** Corpus: whether a concept map's structure tracks the text's paragraph/rhetorical structure [[sources/18_63]]; structure-aligned organizers [[sources/ej1486502]]. Illustrative: opening with "This section makes three claims: …".

### L6 — Narrativity / genre  *(text-side)*
**What.** Narrative (story) vs expository (information) framing of the same content.
**Operationalized as.** Text type; narrative vs expository recasting. → [[concepts/reading-purpose-and-goal]] (genre interacts with goal).
**Example.** Corpus: narrative vs expository text type effects [[sources/journal-of-educational-psychology-1999]]; recasting identical content as narrative vs expository [[sources/ssrn-5678368]]. Illustrative: a physics concept told as a character's story vs a textbook explanation.
