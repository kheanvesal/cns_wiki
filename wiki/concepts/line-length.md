# Line Length / Characters-Per-Line (cpl)

**What it is.** The horizontal extent of a text line. The functional variable is **characters per line (cpl)**, not physical width — see [[concepts/characters-per-line]].

**Evidence.**
- **cpl is the critical mediator** of line-length effects on screen; column/window effects interact with it [[sources/layout-on-screen]].
- A **medium** line length (~55 cpl) gave best comprehension; extremes hurt [[sources/dyson-haselgrove-2001-speed-linelength-screen]].
- Line length influences scrolling amount in proofreading [[sources/chan-2014-line-length-spacing-proofreading]].
- Classic print optima: moderate line length read fastest [[sources/tinker-1963-legibility-of-print]]; craft norm ~45–75 chars [[sources/bringhurst-elements-of-typographic-style]].
- **Century-scale review: preferences are "highly dispersed," with a mean legible line length of ≈100–120 mm** [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]] — reported in **mm**, not characters.
- **The origin of the 45–75 cpl figure.** [[sources/nanavati-bias-2005-optimal-line-length]] is the corpus's source for that range, and the figure has been repeated downstream ever since as though it were a measured threshold. It is a narrative review's guideline, and Nanavati & Bias themselves report "no significant difference" between 35, 55, 75 and 95 cpl — the recommended range's own lower and upper bounds fall *inside* a tested null. **Do not cite 45–75 as an empirical optimum.**
- **The only source that measures line length as a proofreading outcome rather than a speed or readability proxy.** [[sources/porte-2001-typographical-error-salience-l2]] varied line length alone (75/60/45/35 cpl, font, size, spacing, margins all held constant) and found an **inverted U**: error recognition rose as lines narrowed to ≈45 cpl (grand mean 7.67/15; ω² = .42; F = 15.293, p < .001), then **fell at ≈35 cpl** (C3–C4 significant, P = .031). Porte's own explanation — narrowing helps until the text is "mutilated" — is a post-hoc reading of a single 15-person cell.

**Convergence.** Empirical (~55 cpl) and craft (~45–75 chars) optima roughly agree: avoid very long or very short lines. The review's ≈100–120 mm is *not* directly comparable to either, because physical width depends on typeface and type size — the same line-length value expressed in mm, characters, or x-heights designates different amounts of text.

## Where the three unit systems disagree
The corpus now contains **three incompatible line-length recommendations**, and they are not interchangeable:

| Source | Recommendation | Unit | Task | Optimality evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[sources/nanavati-bias-2005-optimal-line-length]] | 45–75 | cpl | general reading | narrative review; internal null at 35/55/75/95 |
| [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]] | 100–120 | **mm** | print/textbooks | preference dispersion review |
| [[sources/dyson-haselgrove-2001-speed-linelength-screen]] | ~55 | cpl | screen comprehension | single study |
| [[sources/porte-2001-typographical-error-salience-l2]] | **≈45** (peak) | cpl | **L2 proofreading** | ω² = .42, inverted U, n = 15/cell |
| [[sources/bringhurst-elements-of-typographic-style]] | 45–75 | cpl | craft norm | — |

**The one real convergence** is at the *lower edge*: Porte peaks at ≈45 cpl, and 45 is the bottom of both the craft norm and Nanavati & Bias's range. Two sources a century apart, on opposite sides of the print/digital divide and in different reading tasks, land on the same lower bound. That is worth more than the widely repeated 45–75 midpoint.

**The upper bound has no support.** Nothing in the corpus tests whether line length *above* 55 cpl is harmful in a controlled way; the 75–95 region is supported only by craft norms and by a study that reported no difference.

## ⚠️ Measurement standard
Tarasov et al. argue that uncoordinated measuring units are "a big drawback" of the whole field, and require line length to be reported in mm alongside x-height, full typeface, and column setting [[sources/tarasov-legibility-of-textbooks-2015]]. **No study in this corpus reports all three.** Line-length optima across these sources therefore cannot be pooled.
